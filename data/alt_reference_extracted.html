01. Fund Structures, Fee Waterfalls, Hurdle Rates & High-Water Marks

  • Investment Methods: Direct (sole control, zero GP fees, massive minimum capital/expertise); Co-Investment (LP invests alongside GP with reduced/zero fees on co-invest sleeve); Fund Investing (GP/LP blind pool, 2 & 20 fee drag).
  • Hurdle Rates: Hard hurdle pays incentive fee ONLY on return above hurdle; Soft hurdle unlocks incentive fee on the entire return once cleared (often paired with a GP catch-up clause in PE!).
  • High-Water Mark (HWM): Prevents GP from charging double performance fees while merely recovering past drawdowns.
  • Clawback & Distribution Waterfalls: Deal-by-deal (American) pays GP carried interest early per exited deal (requires escrow/clawback protection for LPs); Whole-of-fund (European) returns 100% of total committed/paid-in capital + preferred hurdle before GP receives carried interest!

Worked Numerical 1: Hedge Fund Net Return with Hard vs. Soft Hurdle

Fund starts at 100.0M USD, rises +18.0% gross to 118.0M USD. Terms: 2% management fee on ending AUM (independent of incentive fee), 20% incentive fee net of management fee, 6.0% hurdle rate.

  • Management Fee $= 2\% \times 118.0\text{M} = 2.36\text{M USD}$, leaving $NAV_{\text{pre-inc}} = 115.64\text{M USD}$.
  • Hard Hurdle (6M): Excess gain $= 115.64 - 100 - 6.00 = 9.64\text{M}$. Incentive fee $= 20\% \times 9.64\text{M} = 1.928\text{M USD}$. Net Ending NAV $= 113.712\text{M USD}$ ($+ 13.71\%$ net).
  • Soft Hurdle: Since gain $15.64\text{M} > 6.00\text{M}$, incentive fee applies to full $15.64\text{M}$ gain $= 3.128\text{M USD}$ ($+12.51\%$ net).

02. Private Capital: Private Equity, Venture Capital & Private Credit

PE Performance Multiples & Venture Capital Valuation Method
$$DPI = \frac{\text{Cumulative Distributions}}{\text{Paid-In Capital}}, \qquad RVPI = \frac{\text{Residual NAV}}{\text{Paid-In Capital}}, \qquad TVPI = DPI + RVPI$$ $$\text{Post-Money} = \frac{\text{Exit Terminal Value}}{(1 + IRR_{\text{target}})^T}, \qquad \text{Pre-Money} = \text{Post-Money} - \text{Investment}, \qquad \text{Ownership} = \frac{\text{Inv}}{\text{Post}}$$

The J-Curve Effect: Early negative cash flows (drawdowns, management fees on committed capital, upfront write-downs) transition into steep positive realizations in Years 4–8. Private Credit spans direct lending, mezzanine debt (with warrants), and distressed debt (loan-to-own).

Worked Numerical 2: PE Fund Multiples & Venture Capital Equity Ownership

PE Multiples: Paid-in capital $= 85\text{M}$, cumulative LP distributions $= 68\text{M}$, remaining NAV $= 76.5\text{M}$. $DPI = 0.80\times$, $RVPI = 0.90\times$, $TVPI = 1.70\times$.

VC Method: Startup projects Year 5 EBITDA of 12M USD at 10x exit ($TV = 120\text{M}$). VC invests 8.0M USD at a 40% target IRR: $\text{Post-Money} = 120 / 1.40^5 = 22.31\text{M USD}$, $\text{Pre-Money} = 14.31\text{M USD}$, VC ownership $= 8 / 22.31 = 35.85\%$.

03. Direct Commercial Real Estate Valuation & Debt Sizing (L2)

NOI, Direct Capitalization, DCF Reversion & Debt Sizing (LTV / DSCR)
$$V_0 = \frac{NOI_1}{R_{\text{cap}}}, \qquad R_{\text{cap}} = r - g, \qquad \text{Terminal Value}_N = \frac{NOI_{N+1}}{R_{\text{terminal cap}}}$$ $$DSCR = \frac{NOI_1}{\text{Annual Debt Service}}, \qquad LTV = \frac{\text{Mortgage Principal}}{\text{Appraised Value}}$$

Worked Numerical 3: Direct Cap Valuation & Binding Lender Constraint (LTV vs. DSCR)

Office building generates $NOI_1 = 1,250,000\text{ USD}$ at a 6.25% market cap rate: $V_0 = 20,000,000\text{ USD}$. Bank permits max $LTV = 70\%$ (max loan $= 14,000,000\text{ USD}$) AND minimum $DSCR = 1.25\times$ at a 7.50% annual mortgage constant (max debt service $= 1,000,000\text{ USD} \implies$ max loan $= 13,333,333\text{ USD}$). The DSCR constraint binds at 13,333,333 USD!

04. Public REITs: NAVPS, FFO & AFFO Valuation (L2)

Net Asset Value Per Share (NAVPS), FFO & AFFO
$$NAVPS = \frac{(NOI_{\text{cash, fwd}} / R_{\text{cap}}) + \text{Cash/Other Assets} - \text{Total Debt/Liabilities}}{\text{Diluted Shares}}$$ $$FFO = \text{GAAP Net Income} + \text{RE Depreciation} - \text{Gains on Property Sales} + \text{Losses}$$ $$AFFO = FFO - \text{Non-Cash Straight-Line Rent} - \text{Maintenance Capex / Leasing Commissions}$$

Worked Numerical 4: REIT NAV Per Share vs. Market Price

A retail REIT forecasts cash NOI of 48.0M USD (market cap rate $= 6.0\%$), holds 15.0M USD cash, owes 315.0M USD debt across 20.0M shares. Gross real estate $= 48 / 0.06 = 800.0\text{M}$. $NAV = 800 + 15 - 315 = 500.0\text{M} \implies NAVPS = 25.00\text{ USD/share}$. If REIT trades at 22.50 USD, it trades at a 10% discount to NAV.

05. Real Assets: Infrastructure, Timberland & Farmland

  • Infrastructure: Economic (toll roads, airports, ports, grid) vs. Social (hospitals, schools); Greenfield (construction/development risk, higher return) vs. Brownfield (existing cash-flowing assets, lower risk); Availability-based PPPs vs. Demand-based concessions.
  • Timberland: Unique biological growth driver (~60% of return comes from trees physically growing in volume and upgrading from pulpwood to sawtimber—allowing owners to delay harvest when lumber prices slump!).
  • Farmland: Row crops (annual flexibility) vs. Permanent crops (orchards/vineyards—higher upfront capex, multi-year price lock-in).

06. Hedge Fund Strategies, Factor Risks & Fund-of-Funds

  • Equity Hedge: Market Neutral (beta = 0, pairs/quant), Long/Short Equity, Short Bias.
  • Event-Driven: Merger Arbitrage (long target, short acquirer in stock deals—payoff looks like writing a short put / crash risk if deal breaks!), Distressed Securities.
  • Relative Value: Convertible Arbitrage (long convertible bond + short underlying stock Δ-hedged), Fixed Income Arbitrage (high leverage, liquidity squeeze risk).
  • Macro & Managed Futures (CTA): Trend-following / global FX-rate-commodity plays; provides crisis alpha (positive skewness during equity crashes).
  • Conditional Factor Risk: Standard linear regression hides crisis correlations—use conditional dummy variables ($D_{\text{crisis}}$) to capture non-linear tail risk!

07. Commodities & Futures Term Structure (L1 & L2)

Commodity Futures Pricing & Total Return Decomposition
$$F_0(T) \approx S_0 e^{(r + c - \gamma)T} \quad (\text{Backwardation: } F_0 < S_0 \text{ when } \gamma > r + c)$$ $$\text{Total Return} = \text{Collateral Return (T-Bill)} + \text{Price Return} + \text{Roll Return}$$ $$\text{Roll Return} = \frac{\text{Near Futures} - \text{Far Futures}}{\text{Near Futures}} \quad (\text{Positive in Backwardation, Negative in Contango})$$
  • Theories of Futures Returns: Insurance Theory (Keynes: producers hedge by selling futures below expected spot → normal backwardation); Hedging Pressure Hypothesis (both producers & consumers hedge); Theory of Storage (high inventory → low convenience yield → contango; scarce inventory → high convenience yield → backwardation).

Worked Numerical 5: Commodity Futures Total Return Attribution

A fully collateralized crude oil futures index earns T-Bill collateral yield of 4.50%, spot oil rises +3.00%, and the futures curve is in backwardation (expiring near contract at 72.00 USD rolls into next contract at 70.50 USD). Roll yield $= \frac{72.00 - 70.50}{72.00} = +2.08\%$. Total return $\approx 4.50\% + 3.00\% + 2.08\% = 9.58\%$.

08. Digital Assets, Shorting Alternatives & Sustainability (CBOK IX)

  • Digital Assets & Tokenization: Cryptocurrencies (Bitcoin PoW, Proof-of-Stake chains, permissioned vs. permissionless ledgers), Smart Contracts, Tokenization of illiquid assets (fractional real estate/PE ownership improving liquidity & settlement speed). Risks: regulatory shifts, custody key loss, 51% consensus attacks, extreme volatility & episodic correlation spikes during risk-off crashes.
  • Shorting Alternatives: Direct shorting of private PE/RE/hedge funds is structurally impossible due to lock-ups & lack of borrow; instead investors short public proxies (listed REITs, BDCs, commodity futures, or buy CDS/TRS).
  • ESG in Alternatives: PE sponsors drive operational decarbonization during ownership; green buildings earn rental/cap-rate premiums ("green alpha") vs. brown-asset stranding discounts.
