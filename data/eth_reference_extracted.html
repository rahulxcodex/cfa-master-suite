01. Ethics, Trust & The CFA Institute Code of Ethics

Capital markets depend on public trust. When investors lose confidence in fair dealing and fiduciary integrity, risk premiums spike, liquidity dries up, and capital allocation becomes inefficient. The CFA Institute Professional Conduct Program applies uniformly to all CFA charterholders, regular/affiliate members, and CFA Program candidates worldwide.

The Six Tenets of the Code of Ethics

  1. Integrity, Competence, Diligence & Respect: Act with integrity, competence, diligence, respect, and in an ethical manner with the public, clients, prospective clients, employers, employees, colleagues in the investment profession, and other participants in global capital markets.
  2. Client & Profession Primacy: Place the integrity of the investment profession and the interests of clients above their own personal interests.
  3. Independent Professional Judgment: Use reasonable care and exercise independent professional judgment when conducting investment analysis, making investment recommendations, taking investment actions, and engaging in other professional activities.
  4. Encourage Ethical Culture: Practice and encourage others to practice in a professional and ethical manner that will reflect credit on themselves and the profession.
  5. Capital Market Integrity: Promote the integrity and viability of the global capital markets for the ultimate benefit of society.
  6. Maintain & Improve Competence: Maintain and improve their professional competence and strive to maintain and improve the competence of other investment professionals.

Solved Case Study: Standard I(A) Strictest-Law Rule & Dissociation

Facts: Maya Lin, CFA, works in Country X where insider trading by non-officers is not illegal, and local privacy statutes forbid disclosing client tax IDs. Her supervisor asks her to trade on a tipped earnings release before publication and sign a marketing brochure claiming guaranteed 12% annual returns.

Ruling: (1) Maya must refuse to trade because Standard II(A) is stricter than Country X's permissive insider trading law (Standard I(A) Strictest Rule). (2) She must refuse to sign the brochure (Standard I(C) prohibits guaranteeing equity/risky returns) and escalate to compliance; if the firm proceeds anyway, she must dissociate in writing from the brochure and team.

03. Standard II: Integrity of Capital Markets (II(A)–II(B))

  • II(A) Material Nonpublic Information (MNPI): Members and candidates who possess MNPI that could affect the value of an investment must not act or cause others to act on the information.
    • Materiality: Information a reasonable investor would want to know before investing, or likely to significantly affect market price (earnings surprises, M&A, FDA approval, major CEO resignation, or an influential analyst's own unpublished rating change!).
    • Nonpublic: Not yet disseminated in a manner making it generally available to the marketplace (a selective conference call for 15 top clients is still nonpublic; a press release on Bloomberg/SEC EDGAR is public).
    • Mosaic Theory (Permitted!): Combining public information + nonmaterial nonpublic information (e.g., interviewing store managers, counting trucks in a factory parking lot, surveying suppliers) to reach a perceptive analytical conclusion is legal and encouraged—even if the resulting conclusion is material to your thesis!
    • Inadvertent Receipt: If you accidentally receive MNPI (e.g., overhearing executives in an elevator), do NOT trade or tip anyone; report to Compliance to place the security on the Restricted List and encourage public dissemination.
    • Firewalls: Separate Investment Banking/Corporate Finance from Research & Trading via Watch Lists, Restricted Lists, physical/IT barriers, and chaperoned wall-crossings.
  • II(B) Market Manipulation: Must not engage in practices that distort prices or artificially inflate trading volume with the intent to mislead market participants.
    • Information-Based Manipulation: Spreading false rumors on social media, short-and-distort, pump-and-dump, fake press releases.
    • Transaction-Based Manipulation: Wash trades (buying/selling to oneself to fake volume), spoofing/layering (placing limit orders intending to cancel before execution), cornering a market/squeeze, marking the close (window dressing near closing bell).
    • Permitted Trading (NOT Manipulation): Legitimate tax-loss harvesting, executing arbitrage between spot and futures, or liquidating a genuine large position (even if it moves price) when motivated by genuine economic/portfolio objectives rather than intent to deceive.

Solved Case Study: Mosaic Theory vs. Selective Disclosure

Facts: Retail analyst Elena visits 30 regional warehouses of MegaMart, interviews store clerks about inventory shelves (nonmaterial operational feedback), tracks public satellite parking-lot imagery, and calls MegaMart's CFO. During the call, the CFO slips and tells Elena: 'Between us, Q3 EPS will beat consensus by 0.45 USD and we are acquiring RivalCo next Tuesday.'

Ruling: Up until the CFO's call, Elena was executing valid Mosaic Theory. However, once the CFO disclosed upcoming earnings beat and an unannounced M&A deal, Elena possesses MNPI. Trading or publishing her report prior to public announcement violates Standard II(A). She must immediately notify Compliance, place MegaMart on the Restricted List, and encourage MegaMart to issue a public press release.

04. Standard III: Duties to Clients (III(A)–III(E))

Sub-StandardGoverning StandardHigh-Probability Exam Traps
III(A) Loyalty, Prudence & CareAct for the benefit of clients and place clients' interests before employer's or own interests.Soft Dollars (Client Brokerage): Brokerage commissions belong to the client, not the manager. Soft dollars may ONLY purchase research/goods that directly assist the investment decision-making process (e.g., Bloomberg research, quant data)—NEVER firm rent, furniture, travel, or marketing! Also vote proxies responsibly on material governance issues and know who the "client" is (for a pension plan, the plan beneficiaries, not corporate management!).
III(B) Fair DealingDeal fairly and objectively with all clients when providing investment analysis, recommendations, or taking investment actions."Fairly" means disseminate recommendations simultaneously, NOT identically—premium institutional tiers can receive extra customized follow-up calls AFTER broadcast dissemination, provided all clients had fair access to the core trade/recommendation. For oversubscribed IPOs, allocate pro rata across all suitable clients (never favor top fee-paying accounts or family accounts!).
III(C) SuitabilityMake reasonable inquiry into client's investment experience, risk/return objectives, and financial constraints; update IPS at least annually; judge suitability in total portfolio context.Unsolicited client trade requests that contradict the IPS: explain why unsuitable, document refusal to modify IPS, execute only if not materially harmful (or ask client to sign off / transfer to non-managed account if severely jeopardizing financial health). When managing a pooled fund (mutual fund/ETF), suitability means adhering strictly to the fund's stated mandate/prospectus.
III(D) Performance PresentationMake reasonable efforts to ensure performance information is fair, accurate, and complete.Cannot cherry-pick top-performing accounts, omit terminated accounts (survivorship bias), show simulated/backtested results as actual live track records, or extrapolate short-term returns. Brief presentations are acceptable if full backup disclosures are offered on request.
III(E) Preservation of ConfidentialityKeep information about current, former, and prospective clients confidential unless illegal activity, legal mandate, or client permission applies.Notice: Duty extends to former AND prospective clients! You MAY share confidential client info with CFA Institute's Professional Conduct Program (PCP) during an investigation (PCP keeps it confidential).

05. Standard IV: Duties to Employers (IV(A)–IV(C))

  • IV(A) Loyalty: Act for the benefit of employer; do not deprive employer of skills/abilities, divulge confidential information, or cause harm.
    • Independent Practice: Permitted ONLY if written consent is obtained from the current employer after disclosing services, duration, and compensation. (Preparing to leave—e.g., leasing office space, incorporating an LLC—while on personal time without soliciting clients or taking proprietary files is permitted; soliciting clients BEFORE leaving or taking client lists/spreadsheets violates IV(A)!).
    • Post-Departure: Using general knowledge/skills gained in memory is permitted; taking proprietary models, spreadsheets, or contact files without written permission violates IV(A). Contacting former clients via public directories after departing is compliant unless barred by a valid non-compete agreement.
    • Whistleblowing: Permitted ONLY when aimed at protecting client/market integrity (never for personal gain or revenge).
  • IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements: Must not accept gifts, benefits, compensation, or consideration that competes with or might reasonably be expected to create a conflict of interest with employer's interest unless written consent from ALL parties involved is obtained (both the employer AND the outside compensating party/client!). Notice the exam trap: I(B) allows modest gifts or disclosure for regular incentives, whereas IV(B) requires WRITTEN consent from all parties for extra compensation arrangements (e.g., client offers an analyst a free vacation home or cash bonus for hitting a return hurdle)!
  • IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors: Must make reasonable efforts to ensure that anyone subject to their supervision or authority complies with applicable laws, rules, regulations, and the Code and Standards.
    • Inadequate Compliance System: If the firm's compliance procedures are inadequate, a supervisor who accepts supervisory responsibility still violates IV(C)! Must urge adoption of written policies, delegate carefully, monitor regularly, and refuse supervisory responsibility in writing if senior leadership refuses to install proper controls.
    • After Detecting a Violation: Simply warning the employee is NOT enough! Must (1) investigate thoroughly, (2) restrict/place employee under heightened supervision pending review, and (3) ensure no repeat occurs.

06. Standard V: Investment Analysis, Recommendations & Actions (V(A)–V(C))

  • V(A) Diligence & Reasonable Basis: Exercise diligence, independence, and thoroughness in analyzing investments, making recommendations, and taking investment actions; have a reasonable and adequate basis supported by appropriate research and investigation.
    • Secondary / Third-Party / Quantitative / AI Research: You may rely on external/colleague/LLM/quant models ONLY after verifying the soundness of assumptions, data quality, stress-testing, and limitation boundaries (must NOT blindly trust a consensus buy rating or black-box AI score!).
    • Group Decision Dissent: If a research committee outvotes you, you may allow your name to remain on a consensus report ONLY if you believe the group had a reasonable and adequate basis (even if you disagree with the conclusion). If the process lacked diligence, you must remove your name/dissociate!
  • V(B) Communication with Clients & Prospective Clients:
    • Disclose basic format and general principles of investment processes and promptly disclose any material changes.
    • Disclose significant limitations and risks (liquidity, leverage, model risk, cybersecurity/AI assumptions).
    • Distinguish clearly between Fact and Opinion in presentation of investment analysis and recommendations (e.g., past EPS is fact; projected next-year EPS and target P/E are opinion!).
  • V(C) Record Retention: Develop and maintain appropriate records (hard copy or electronic) to support investment analyses, recommendations, actions, and client communications. CFA Institute recommends a minimum 7-year retention period (unless stricter local law applies). Remember: records created at an employer belong to the employer, so do NOT take original working files when leaving without consent!

07. Standard VI: Conflicts of Interest (VI(A)–VI(C))

  • VI(A) Avoid or Disclose Conflicts (12th Ed. updated wording!): Avoid conflicts of interest when possible, and make full and fair disclosure of all matters that could reasonably be expected to impair independence and objectivity or interfere with respective duties to clients, prospective clients, and employer.
    • Examples: Material beneficial stock ownership in covered companies, board directorships, investment banking relationships, fee-splitting/revenue-sharing, cross-selling incentives.
    • Plain Language: Disclosures must be prominent, specific, and in plain language—not buried in 6-point legal boilerplate!
  • VI(B) Priority of Transactions: Investment transactions for clients and employers must have priority over investment transactions in which a member or candidate is the beneficial owner.
    • Hierarchy: (1) Clients FIRST, (2) Employer SECOND, (3) Personal / Beneficial Family accounts LAST!
    • Beneficial Ownership Trap: Includes accounts of spouse, minor children, or shared household relatives where the member has direct/indirect pecuniary benefit! (Exception: If a fee-paying family member account—e.g. parent's regular retail account—pays the same fees and receives no preferential treatment, treat them identically with other clients under III(B), NOT last!).
    • Compliance Controls: Pre-clearance, blackout periods (e.g., 5 days before/after research), restricted lists, duplicate broker confirms, minimum holding periods (60 days).
  • VI(C) Referral Fees: Disclose to employer, clients, and prospective clients (as appropriate) any compensation, consideration, or benefit received from or paid to others for the recommendation of products or services.
    • Must disclose BEFORE entering into any formal agreement for services!
    • Must specify BOTH the nature (cash, trailing fee, reciprocal brokerage, non-monetary perks) and the estimated dollar/percentage value of the referral arrangement.

08. Standard VII: Responsibilities as a Member or Candidate (VII(A)–VII(B))

  • VII(A) Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs: Must not engage in any conduct that compromises the reputation or integrity of CFA Institute or the CFA designation or the integrity, validity, or security of CFA Program exams.
    • Prohibited: Discussing specific exam questions, formulas tested, or topic weights observed on an exam (even after the testing window closes!), bringing unauthorized items into the exam room, writing during unpermitted intervals, or cheating.
    • Permitted: Expressing general opinions about exam difficulty, curriculum length, or personal study experience ("The exam felt challenging" or "I disliked the testing center temperature" is fine; "There were 3 questions on GARCH and a question asking about LIFO liquidation" violates VII(A)!).
  • VII(B) Reference to CFA Institute, CFA Designation & CFA Program: Must not misrepresent or exaggerate the meaning or implications of membership, holding the CFA designation, or candidacy.
    • Adjective Only, NEVER a Noun!: Correct: "John Doe, CFA" or "John Doe is a CFA charterholder." WRONG: "John Doe is a CFA" or "CFAs outperform the market" or "CFA-certified" or "John Doe, C.F.A."!
    • No Partial Designation: Cannot write "CFA Level II" or "CFA Level III Candidate" as a title/credential beside a name (e.g., "Jane Smith, CFA L2" is VIOLATION). Correct: "Jane Smith is a candidate in the CFA Program" (only valid if actively registered for an upcoming exam!).
    • Superior Returns Claim: Cannot imply holding the charter produces superior performance or guarantees better investment returns.
    • Lapsed Membership: If annual dues or Professional Conduct Statement (PCS) are unpaid/unsubmitted, charterholder status is inactive and you MUST STOP using the CFA designation until reinstated!

09. GIPS® Standards & Asset Manager Code (CBOK I & XII)

2026–2027 Curriculum Note: Standalone GIPS readings were streamlined out of the L1/L2 core sequence for 2027, yet GIPS compliance fundamentals and the Asset Manager Code of Professional Conduct (AMC) remain central pillars of the CFA Institute CBOK (Sections I & XII) and Standard III(D) Performance Presentation.

Core GIPS® Architecture & Calculation Rules

  • Who Can Claim Compliance? Only an entire investment firm defined as a distinct business entity held out to clients (NEVER an individual manager, single composite, or software vendor; NEVER claim "partial compliance").
  • Composites: Must include all actual, fee-paying, discretionary portfolios managed according to a similar investment mandate, objective, or strategy. Non-fee-paying discretionary portfolios may be included if disclosed; non-discretionary portfolios MUST be excluded!
  • Minimum Track Record: Initially present at least 5 years of GIPS-compliant performance (or since inception if <5 years), then build up to at least 10 years of consecutive annual compliant performance.
  • Return Methodology: Must use Time-Weighted Return (TWR) valuing portfolios at fair value (trade-date accounting, accrual accounting for fixed-income interest), asset-weighted across portfolios in the composite using beginning-of-period values (or beginning + cash-flow weights).
  • Verification: Optional (not mandatory for compliance), must be performed by an independent third party on a firm-wide basis (cannot verify a single composite alone!).
GIPS Asset-Weighted Composite Return & Modified Dietz Approximation
$$R_{\text{Composite}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N BMVE_i \cdot R_i}{\sum_{i=1}^N BMVE_i}, \qquad R_{\text{ModDietz}} = \frac{EMV - BMV - CF}{BMV + \sum_{j=1}^k CF_j \cdot w_j}, \quad w_j = \frac{CD - D_j}{CD}$$

Asset Manager Code of Professional Conduct (Firm-Level Standards)

Whereas Standards I–VII bind individual members and candidates, the Asset Manager Code (AMC) establishes 6 ethical principles for investment management firms: (1) Loyalty to Clients, (2) Investment Process & Actions, (3) Trading (best execution & soft-dollar limits), (4) Risk Management, Compliance & Support (independent risk officer, business continuity plan), (5) Performance & Valuation (fair market pricing, GIPS-style clarity), and (6) Disclosures (fees, conflicts, soft dollars, proxy policies).

Worked Numerical Example: GIPS Asset-Weighted Composite Return

Scenario: A composite has two fee-paying discretionary accounts in Q1: Account A starts with 12,000,000 USD and earns +4.20%; Account B starts with 8,000,000 USD and earns +6.70% (no external cash flows).

Calculation: Total Beginning Assets = $12\text{M} + 8\text{M} = 20\text{M USD}$. Composite Return = $(0.60 \times 4.20\%) + (0.40 \times 6.70\%) = 2.52\% + 2.68\% = 5.20\%$ (note: equal-weighted average would wrongly report 5.45%).

Exercise 9.3 (Solved): Modified Dietz Return Calculation
GIPS Math SOLVED WALKTHROUGH

Question: A portfolio begins a 30-day month at 10,000,000 USD, receives a 2,000,000 USD deposit on Day 15 ($w = (30 - 15)/30 = 0.50$), and ends the month at 12,550,000 USD. Calculate the Modified Dietz return.

Solution & Step-by-Step Ruling (Expanded — Click to Collapse)

Solution: $+5.00\%$.

Calculation: Numerator $= 12,550,000 - 10,000,000 - 2,000,000 = 550,000\text{ USD}$. Weighted capital base $= 10,000,000 + (2,000,000 \times 0.50) = 11,000,000\text{ USD}$. Return $= 550,000 / 11,000,000 = 0.0500 = 5.00\%$.
