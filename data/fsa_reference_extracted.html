01. Introduction to FSA & Mechanics

Financial Statement Analysis provides analysts with the quantitative foundation to assess a company's past performance, current financial position, risk profile, and future cash flow generating capabilities.

The Accounting Equation & Double-Entry Accounting

Core Accounting Identity
$$\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Contributed Capital} + \text{Ending Retained Earnings}$$ $$\text{Ending Retained Earnings} = \text{Beginning Retained Earnings} + \text{Net Income} - \text{Dividends Declared}$$

Every transaction affects at least two accounts (debits must equal credits). Under accrual accounting, revenues are recognized when earned and expenses when incurred, decoupling accounting profit from immediate cash inflows and outflows.

The Financial Statement Framework (Core 4 Statements)

  1. Balance Sheet (Statement of Financial Position): Snapshot of what a company owns (assets) and owes (liabilities and equity) at a specific point in time.
  2. Income Statement (Statement of Comprehensive Income): Measures financial performance over a specified duration (revenues minus expenses).
  3. Statement of Cash Flows: Reconciles beginning and ending cash balances across Operating, Investing, and Financing activities over a period.
  4. Statement of Changes in Equity: Details movements in contributed capital, retained earnings, and accumulated other comprehensive income (AOCI).

Auditor Opinions Spectrum

Audit Opinion Meaning / Circumstances Analyst Implication
Unqualified (Clean) Financial statements are presented fairly in all material respects conforming with GAAP/IFRS. High reliability of baseline reporting. Standard baseline for analysis.
Qualified Statements are fairly presented except for a specific, isolated departure or scope limitation. Carefully inspect footnote explanations regarding the exception. Adjust models.
Adverse Financial statements as a whole are materially misstated or non-compliant. Severe Red Flag. Financial data cannot be relied upon for valuation.
Disclaimer of Opinion Auditor is unable to form an opinion due to severe scope limitation or lack of independence. Extreme risk. Company management may be withholding critical solvency data.

Financial reporting standards provide consistency, comparability, and transparency across global capital markets. The two dominant global standard setters are the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB) and the Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB).

⚠️ The Pervasive Cost Constraint

The benefits of financial reporting information must always exceed the costs of providing and using that information. Standard setters do not mandate disclosure if compliance costs outweigh decision-making utility.

03. Understanding Income Statements

The Income Statement reports the financial performance of an entity over a period, reflecting operational revenues, expenses, financing costs, and taxes.

Revenue Recognition: The 5-Step Model (IFRS 15 & ASC 606)

  1. Identify the contract(s) with a customer (commercial substance, rights, and collectability).
  2. Identify the distinct performance obligations in the contract.
  3. Determine the transaction price (considering variable consideration, discounts, and financing terms).
  4. Allocate the transaction price to performance obligations based on relative standalone selling prices.
  5. Recognize revenue when (or as) the entity satisfies a performance obligation (point in time vs. over time).

Earnings Per Share (EPS): Basic and Diluted

Basic EPS
$$\text{Basic EPS} = \frac{\text{Net Income} - \text{Preferred Dividends}}{\text{Weighted Average Number of Ordinary Shares (WACS)}}$$

When potentially dilutive securities (convertible bonds, convertible preferred stock, stock options, warrants) exist, Diluted EPS must be calculated:

Diluted EPS Formula
$$\text{Diluted EPS} = \frac{\text{Net Income} - \text{Pref Div} + \text{Pref Div on Conv Pref} + \text{Conv Debt Interest}(1 - t)}{\text{WACS} + \text{Shares from Conv Pref} + \text{Shares from Conv Debt} + \text{Net Shares from Options (Treasury Stock Method)}}$$
💡 The Treasury Stock Method for Options & Warrants

Assumes proceeds from exercising in-the-money options are used to repurchase shares at the average market price over the period:

$$\Delta \text{Shares} = \text{Options} \times \left(1 - \frac{\text{Exercise Price}}{\text{Average Market Price}}\right)$$

If the calculated Diluted EPS is greater than Basic EPS, the security is anti-dilutive and must be excluded from Diluted EPS reporting.

Comprehensive Income & Other Comprehensive Income (OCI)

Comprehensive Income includes Net Income plus Other Comprehensive Income (OCI). Key items bypassing the Income Statement and entering OCI directly under the PUFE acronym:

  • P: Pension adjustments (actuarial gains/losses and prior service costs under US GAAP; re-measurements under IFRS).
  • U: Unrealized holding gains/losses on available-for-sale (FVOCI) debt securities.
  • F: Foreign currency translation adjustments (Cumulative Translation Adjustment - CTA under Current Rate method).
  • E: Effective portion of cash flow hedge gains/losses.

04. Understanding Balance Sheets

The Balance Sheet displays the financial structure of the firm, revealing liquidity, capital resources, and asset composition.

Measurement Bases of Assets & Liabilities

  • Historical Cost: Initial acquisition consideration plus costs to prepare asset for use (objective, verifiable, but lacks market relevance over time).
  • Amortized Cost: Historical cost adjusted for cumulative depreciation, amortization, depletion, and impairment.
  • Fair Value: Exit price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants.
  • Net Realizable Value (NRV): Estimated selling price in the ordinary course of business less estimated completion and selling costs.

Tangible vs. Intangible Assets & Goodwill

Internally generated intangible assets (e.g., brand value, patents developed in-house):

  • US GAAP: All Research and Development (R&D) costs must be expensed as incurred (with narrow exceptions for software development after technological feasibility).
  • IFRS: Research phase costs are expensed; Development phase costs are capitalized once commercial feasibility, technical viability, and economic benefit are demonstrated.

Goodwill arises only in business combinations when purchase consideration exceeds the fair value of net identifiable assets acquired. Goodwill is never amortized under IFRS or US GAAP; it is tested annually for impairment.

05. Understanding Cash Flow Statements

The Cash Flow Statement eliminates accrual adjustments to show literal cash generated and used across three primary categories: Operating (CFO), Investing (CFI), and Financing (CFF).

Direct vs. Indirect Method for Cash Flow from Operations (CFO)

Method Mechanism Conversion Adjustments
Direct Method Lists gross cash receipts (cash collected from customers) and gross cash disbursements (cash paid to suppliers, employees, interest, taxes). $$\text{Cash Collected} = \text{Sales} - \Delta \text{AR} + \Delta \text{Unearned Rev}$$ $$\text{Cash Paid to Suppliers} = \text{COGS} + \Delta \text{Inv} - \Delta \text{AP}$$
Indirect Method Starts with Net Income, adds back non-cash expenses (depreciation, amortization), adjusts for non-operating items (gains/losses on sale), and adds/subtracts changes in working capital accounts. $$\text{CFO} = \text{Net Income} + \text{Depr/Amort} - \text{Gain on Sale} + \text{Loss on Sale} - \Delta \text{Current Assets} + \Delta \text{Current Liabilities}$$

Classification Differences: IFRS vs. US GAAP

Cash Flow Item US GAAP Classification IFRS Allowed Classifications
Interest Paid Operating (CFO) Operating (CFO) or Financing (CFF)
Interest Received Operating (CFO) Operating (CFO) or Investing (CFI)
Dividends Paid Financing (CFF) Financing (CFF) or Operating (CFO)
Dividends Received Operating (CFO) Operating (CFO) or Investing (CFI)
Taxes Paid Operating (CFO) Operating (unless specific to CFI or CFF)

Free Cash Flow: FCFF and FCFE

Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF)
$$\text{FCFF} = \text{CFO} + \text{Interest Expense} \times (1 - \text{Tax Rate}) - \text{FCInv}$$ $$\text{FCFF} = \text{NI} + \text{NCC} + \text{Int} \times (1 - t) - \text{FCInv} - \text{WCInv}$$
Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE)
$$\text{FCFE} = \text{CFO} - \text{FCInv} + \text{Net Borrowing}$$

06. Financial Analysis Techniques & DuPont

Financial ratios standardize financial statement data to assess liquidity, activity, solvency, profitability, and return metrics across entities regardless of absolute size.

DuPont Return on Equity (ROE) Decomposition

3-Step DuPont Analysis
$$\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\left(\frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Revenue}}\right)}_{\text{Net Profit Margin}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Total Assets}}\right)}_{\text{Asset Turnover}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{\text{Total Assets}}{\text{Equity}}\right)}_{\text{Financial Leverage Ratio}}$$
5-Step Extended DuPont Analysis
$$\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\left(\frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{EBT}}\right)}_{\text{Tax Burden}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{\text{EBT}}{\text{EBIT}}\right)}_{\text{Interest Burden}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Revenue}}\right)}_{\text{Operating Margin}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Total Assets}}\right)}_{\text{Asset Turnover}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{\text{Total Assets}}{\text{Equity}}\right)}_{\text{Financial Leverage}}$$

Key Ratio Reference Handbook

Classification Ratio Name Formula Analytical Interpretation
Liquidity Current Ratio $\frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$ Short-term debt-paying capability. Normal range: 1.2x – 2.0x.
Quick (Acid-Test) Ratio $\frac{\text{Cash} + \text{Marketable Securities} + \text{Receivables}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$ Excludes illiquid inventory and prepaid expenses.

Interest Coverage (Times Interest Earned) $\frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Interest Payments}}$ Ability to service periodic debt interest obligations out of earnings.

07. Inventories (FIFO, LIFO & Valuation)

Inventory valuation significantly influences Cost of Goods Sold (COGS), gross margins, taxable income, and balance sheet working capital.

Cost Flow Methods Under Inflationary Conditions

  • FIFO (First-In, First-Out): Assigns earliest costs to COGS; ending inventory reflects recent market prices. Under inflation: lower COGS $\rightarrow$ higher Net Income $\rightarrow$ higher income taxes $\rightarrow$ lower operating cash flow.
  • LIFO (Last-In, First-Out): Assigns latest costs to COGS; ending inventory reflects older historic prices. Permitted only under US GAAP (strictly prohibited under IFRS). Under inflation: higher COGS $\rightarrow$ lower Net Income $\rightarrow$ lower taxes $\rightarrow$ higher operating cash flow (tax savings).
  • Weighted Average Cost: Assigns average unit cost across all inventory available for sale. Results fall strictly between FIFO and LIFO.
Visual 2: Financial Statement Impact under Inflation (Rising Prices)
Comparison of financial outcomes across FIFO, Weighted Average, and LIFO cost methods.

LIFO Reserve and LIFO Liquidation Formulas

To compare a LIFO-reporting firm with a FIFO competitor, analysts must adjust using the LIFO Reserve disclosed in footnotes:

LIFO to FIFO Conversions
$$\text{Inventory}_{\text{FIFO}} = \text{Inventory}_{\text{LIFO}} + \text{LIFO Reserve}$$ $$\text{COGS}_{\text{FIFO}} = \text{COGS}_{\text{LIFO}} - \Delta \text{LIFO Reserve}$$ $$\text{Retained Earnings}_{\text{FIFO}} = \text{Retained Earnings}_{\text{LIFO}} + \text{LIFO Reserve} \times (1 - \text{Tax Rate})$$
🚨 LIFO Liquidation Risk

Occurs when inventory unit sales exceed unit purchases, forcing older, lower-cost inventory layers into COGS. This artificially compresses COGS, generates an unsustainable spike in gross profit and net income, and creates a substantial income tax liability.

Lower of Cost and Net Realizable Value / Market

Standard Measurement Floor / Ceiling Impairment Reversal Allowed?
IFRS Lower of Cost or Net Realizable Value (NRV = Estimated Sales Price - Completion/Selling Costs). Yes (Permitted up to the original cost amount).
US GAAP (FIFO / Weighted Avg) Lower of Cost or Net Realizable Value (NRV). No (Strictly prohibited).
US GAAP (LIFO / Retail) Lower of Cost or Market (Market = Replacement cost bounded by Ceiling [NRV] and Floor [NRV - Normal Profit Margin]). No (Strictly prohibited).

08. Long-Lived Assets & Depreciation

Accounting for property, plant, and equipment (PPE) and intangible assets impacts asset base size, operating margins, and cash flow classifications.

Capitalizing vs. Expensing Costs

Financial Metric Capitalizing Expenditure Expensing Immediately
Initial Net Income Higher (cost deferred via balance sheet) Lower (entire cost hits P&L immediately)
Subsequent Net Income Lower (due to ongoing depreciation/amortization) Higher (no future depreciation burden)
Cash Flow from Operations (CFO) Higher (cash outflow classified as CFI) Lower (cash outflow classified as CFO)
Total Assets & Equity Higher in initial years Lower in initial years

Depreciation Mechanics

Straight-Line Depreciation
$$\text{Annual Expense} = \frac{\text{Historical Cost} - \text{Residual Value}}{\text{Useful Life}}$$
Double-Declining Balance (DDB) Depreciation
$$\text{Expense}_t = \text{Carrying Value at Start of Year } t \times \left(\frac{2}{\text{Useful Life}}\right)$$ (Note: Salvage value is not deducted when computing annual expense, but asset is never depreciated below salvage value).
Visual 3: Carrying Value Trajectories by Depreciation Method
Asset carrying amount over 5-year useful life (Cost: 100,000 USD; Salvage: 10,000 USD).

IFRS Revaluation Model vs. US GAAP Cost Model

Under IFRS, companies may choose the Revaluation Model for entire asset classes:

  • If revaluation increases carrying value: Initial gain bypasses P&L and goes directly to Revaluation Surplus in OCI (unless reversing a prior loss recognized in P&L).
  • If revaluation decreases carrying value: Loss is recognized in P&L to the extent it exceeds previous revaluation surplus in OCI.
  • US GAAP strictly mandates the Cost Model; revaluation upward to fair value is prohibited.

Impairment of Long-Lived Assets

  • IFRS: Impairment occurs when Carrying Value exceeds Recoverable Amount (Higher of: Fair Value less costs to sell, and Value in Use [PV of future cash flows]). Impairment write-downs can be reversed up to historical cost.
  • US GAAP: 2-step test. Step 1 (Recoverability test): If undiscounted future cash flows $<$ Carrying Value, asset is impaired. Step 2 (Loss measurement): Impairment Loss = Carrying Value $-$ Fair Value. Impairment write-downs for assets held for use cannot be reversed.

09. Income Taxes (DTAs, DTLs & Tax Rates)

Differences between accounting rules (IFRS/US GAAP) and tax legislation generate disparities between Accounting Profit and Taxable Income.

Core Tax Terminology

  • Income Tax Expense: P&L tax expense = Current Tax Payable $+$ $\Delta$DTL $-$ $\Delta$DTA.
  • Current Tax Payable: Actual cash liability owed to tax authorities based on Taxable Income.
  • Deferred Tax Asset (DTA): Future tax savings created when Taxable Income $>$ Accounting Profit (e.g., tax paid early, warranty accruals, loss carryforwards).
  • Deferred Tax Liability (DTL): Future tax payable created when Accounting Profit $>$ Taxable Income (e.g., accelerated tax depreciation vs straight-line book depreciation).
Balance Sheet Approach to Deferred Taxes
$$\text{Temporary Difference} = \text{Carrying Value (Book)} - \text{Tax Base}$$ $$\text{If Book Asset} > \text{Tax Base Asset} \implies \mathbf{DTL}$$ $$\text{If Book Asset} < \text{Tax Base Asset} \implies \mathbf{DTA}$$ $$\text{If Book Liability} > \text{Tax Base Liability} \implies \mathbf{DTA}$$ $$\text{If Book Liability} < \text{Tax Base Liability} \implies \mathbf{DTL}$$

Valuation Allowances & Tax Rate Revisions

Under US GAAP, a Valuation Allowance contra-account reduces DTA if it is "more likely than not" ($>50\%$ probability) that some or all of the DTA will not be realized through future taxable profits. (IFRS reduces DTA carrying value directly).

⚠️ Effect of Tax Rate Changes on Existing DTA/DTL

When the enacted statutory tax rate decreases:

  • Existing DTL balances decrease $\rightarrow$ Increases Net Income in P&L.
  • Existing DTA balances decrease $\rightarrow$ Decreases Net Income in P&L.

10. Non-Current Liabilities & Bonds

Corporate debt financing through bonds payable involves tracking coupon payments, interest expense, and liability amortization.

Bond Issuance Pricing Dynamics

  • Par Bond: Coupon Rate $=$ Market Yield at Issuance (YTM). Book Value remains at Face Value.
  • Discount Bond: Coupon Rate $<$ Market Yield. Issued below par; Carrying value increases over life toward par. Interest Expense $>$ Coupon Paid.
  • Premium Bond: Coupon Rate $>$ Market Yield. Issued above par; Carrying value decreases over life toward par. Interest Expense $<$ Coupon Paid.
Effective Interest Rate Method
$$\text{Periodic Interest Expense} = \text{Carrying Value at Start of Period} \times \text{Market Yield at Issuance}$$ $$\text{Coupon Paid} = \text{Face (Par) Value} \times \text{Stated Coupon Rate}$$ $$\Delta \text{Carrying Value} = \text{Interest Expense} - \text{Coupon Paid}$$
Visual 4: Bond Carrying Value Convergence to Par
Evolution of Carrying Amount over 5 years for Discount (8% coupon, 10% yield) vs Premium (12% coupon, 10% yield) bonds.

Debt Derecognition (Extinguishment)

When debt is retired early before maturity:

$$\text{Gain or Loss on Extinguishment} = \text{Carrying Value of Debt} - \text{Reacquisition Price Paid}$$

Recognized as an ordinary gain or loss in the Income Statement.

12. Financial Reporting Quality Spectrum (L1)

Financial reporting quality spans a wide spectrum, ranging from pristine economic truth to outright fraudulent fabrication.

Quality Tier Characteristics
Tier 1: High Quality / High Earnings Quality GAAP/IFRS compliant, transparent, decision-useful, faithful representation, sustainable economic earnings.
Tier 2: High Quality / Low Earnings Quality GAAP/IFRS compliant and transparent, but earnings have low sustainability (high cyclicality, volatile margins, non-recurring items).
Tier 3: Biased Accounting Choices Within GAAP/IFRS rules, but aggressive (early revenue recognition, delayed expenses) or conservative (cookie-jar reserves) choices obscure performance.
Tier 4: Earnings Management Deliberate timing of real transactions or aggressive accounting discretion to manipulate reported metrics.
Tier 5: Non-Compliant / Fraudulent Departures from GAAP/IFRS, fabricated fictitious revenues, hidden liabilities, and intentional fraud.

13. Intercorporate Investments (CFA L2)

At Level 2, intercorporate investments are evaluated based on the degree of influence or control the investor company exercises over the investee.

Investment Classification Matrix

Ownership Level Typical Influence Accounting Standard / Method
$< 20\%$ Passive (No Significant Influence) IFRS 9 / ASC 321: Amortized Cost, FVOCI, or FVPL
20% – 50% Significant Influence Equity Method (One-line consolidation)
$> 50\%$ Control Acquisition Method (Full 100% consolidation)
Variable Interest Primary Beneficiary VIE / SPE Consolidation (regardless of voting rights)

The Equity Method Mechanics (20% – 50%)

Reported on the balance sheet as a single non-current asset line:

$$\text{Ending Investment Value} = \text{Initial Cost} + (\% \times \text{Investee NI}) - (\% \times \text{Investee Dividends}) - \text{Amortization of Excess Purchase Price}$$

Note: Dividends received under the equity method do not impact the investor's Net Income; they are treated as a return of capital, reducing the carrying value of the investment and appearing as CFO cash inflow.

Business Combinations: Acquisition Method ($>50\%$)

All assets and liabilities of the subsidiary are consolidated at 100% Fair Value on acquisition date. If less than 100% is purchased, Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) is established.

Goodwill Approach IFRS vs US GAAP Goodwill Calculation Formula NCI Measurement Base
Full Goodwill Permitted under IFRS; Mandatory under US GAAP $$\text{Fair Value of Subsidiary as Whole} - \text{FV of Identifiable Net Assets}$$ NCI measured at Fair Value of minority shares
Partial Goodwill Permitted under IFRS; Prohibited under US GAAP $$\text{Purchase Price Paid} - (\% \times \text{FV of Identifiable Net Assets})$$ NCI measured at minority % share of FV of net identifiable assets

15. Multinational Operations & Currency Translation (CFA L2)

Analyzing global corporations requires translating foreign subsidiary accounts denominated in local currency into the parent firm's reporting presentation currency.

Three Functional Currency Designations

  • Presentation (Reporting) Currency: The currency in which the parent entity prepares its consolidated financial statements.

16. Analysis of Financial Institutions (CFA L2)

Financial institutions (banks, insurance companies) hold highly leveraged balance sheets comprised mostly of financial contracts, requiring specialized analytic frameworks.

The CAMELS Bank Evaluation Framework

  • C - Capital Adequacy: Risk-weighted capital adequacy ratios (Basel III standards).
  • A - Asset Quality: Credit risk, Non-Performing Loans (NPLs), allowance for loan losses.
  • M - Management Capabilities: Risk governance, internal controls, regulatory compliance track record.
  • E - Earnings Quality: High Net Interest Margin (NIM), sustainable fee-based income, low reliance on non-recurring trading gains.
  • L - Liquidity Position: Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) and Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR).
  • S - Sensitivity to Market Risk: Asset-liability duration matching and Value-at-Risk (VaR) exposures.

Basel III Regulatory Capital Structure

Basel III Minimum Capital Ratios (relative to Risk-Weighted Assets - RWA)
$$\text{Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1)} \ge 4.5\%$$ $$\text{Total Tier 1 Capital (CET1 + Additional Tier 1)} \ge 6.0\%$$ $$\text{Total Capital (Tier 1 + Tier 2 Capital)} \ge 8.0\%$$

Insurance Company Key Performance Indicators

Property & Casualty (P&C) Underwriting Ratios
$$\text{Loss Ratio} = \frac{\text{Claims Paid} + \Delta \text{Loss Reserves}}{\text{Net Premiums Earned}}$$ $$\text{Expense Ratio} = \frac{\text{Underwriting \& Acquisition Expenses}}{\text{Net Premiums Written}}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Combined Ratio}} = \text{Loss Ratio} + \text{Expense Ratio}$$

A Combined Ratio $< 100\%$ indicates underwriting profitability before taking into account investment returns on float.

17. Evaluating Quality of Financial Reports & Beneish M-Score

Forensic financial analysis evaluates whether reported numbers reflect genuine underlying economics or manager distortion.

The Beneish M-Score Model for Earnings Manipulation

A probit model that evaluates 8 quantitative metrics to estimate the probability that a company has manipulated its earnings. An M-Score greater than $-1.78$ (e.g., $-1.20$) indicates a high probability of manipulation.

Variable Name & Calculation Manipulation Red Flag Condition
DSRI Days Sales in Receivables Index: $\frac{\text{DSO}_t}{\text{DSO}_{t-1}}$ $>1.0$: Receivables growing faster than sales (channel stuffing, unearned revenue booking).
GMI Gross Margin Index: $\frac{\text{Gross Margin}_{t-1}}{\text{Gross Margin}_t}$ $>1.0$: Deteriorating margins create pressure to engage in earnings manipulation.
AQI Asset Quality Index: $\frac{[1 - (\text{Current Assets}_t + \text{PPE}_t + \text{Securities}_t) / \text{Total Assets}_t]}{[1 - (\text{Current Assets}_{t-1} + \text{PPE}_{t-1} + \text{Securities}_{t-1}) / \text{Total Assets}_{t-1}]}$ $>1.0$: Increasing proportion of non-current intangible/deferred costs capitalized on balance sheet.
SGI Sales Growth Index: $\frac{\text{Sales}_t}{\text{Sales}_{t-1}}$ $>1.0$: High growth companies face enormous market pressure when growth slows.
DEPI Depreciation Index: $\frac{\text{Depreciation Rate}_{t-1}}{\text{Depreciation Rate}_t}$ $>1.0$: Slowdown in depreciation rate indicates extension of asset lives to boost earnings.
SGAI SG&A Expense Index: $\frac{(\text{SG\&A}_t / \text{Sales}_t)}{(\text{SG\&A}_{t-1} / \text{Sales}_{t-1})}$ $>1.0$: Decreasing administrative efficiency.
LVGI Leverage Index: $\frac{\text{Leverage}_t}{\text{Leverage}_{t-1}}$ $>1.0$: Increasing debt relative to assets creates risk of covenant breaches.
TATA Total Accruals to Total Assets: $\frac{\text{Net Income}_t - \text{CFO}_t}{\text{Total Assets}_t}$ High positive value indicates earnings are driven by non-cash accruals rather than cash collections.

18. Integration of FSA Techniques

Synthesizing analysis across multiple financial statements to normalize metrics and build reliable multi-period valuation forecasts.

Framework for Normalizing Financial Statements

  1. Inventory Normalization: Convert LIFO inventory and COGS to FIFO values using the LIFO Reserve.
  2. Off-Balance-Sheet & Operating Lease Adjustments: Bring historical commitments onto the balance sheet.
  3. Pension Adjustments: Reclassify operating vs financing pension components; eliminate corridor smoothing for US GAAP.
  4. Goodwill & Intangible Adjustments: Subtract goodwill from equity to calculate Tangible Book Value ($TBV$) for conservative leverage analysis.
  5. Non-Recurring Items Removal: Strip out restructuring charges, gains/losses on asset sales, and foreign currency translation spikes when estimating baseline run-rate operating income.
