01. Corporate Structures, Ownership, Governance & ESG Risk Factors

  • Organizational Forms: Sole Proprietorship & General Partnership (unlimited liability, pass-through tax) vs. Limited Partnership (GP unlimited, LP limited) vs. Corporation (separate legal entity, limited liability, double taxation unless S-Corp/pass-through).
  • Shareholder vs. Stakeholder Theories: Principal-agent problems arise between shareholders & managers, and between controlling & minority shareholders (especially via dual-class voting structures).
  • Board Independence & Committees: Independent audit, compensation, and nominating committees mitigate entrenchment; staggered boards protect management from hostile takeovers (negative for minority governance).
  • ESG Materiality: Environmental (carbon transition, stranded assets), Social (labor safety, data privacy), Governance (executive pay alignment, clawbacks).

02. Capital Allocation, NPV, IRR, ROIC & Real Options

Capital Budgeting Metrics & Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)
$$NPV = \sum_{t=0}^N \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t}, \qquad PI = \frac{PV(\text{Future CFs})}{CF_0} = 1 + \frac{NPV}{CF_0}$$ $$ROIC = \frac{NOPAT}{\text{Average Invested Capital}} = \frac{EBIT(1 - t)}{\text{Total Debt + Equity - Excess Cash}}$$

Exam Traps: When NPV and IRR conflict on mutually exclusive projects (due to scale or cash flow timing differences), ALWAYS pick the higher NPV! Non-conventional cash flows (multiple sign changes) produce multiple IRRs. Real options (timing, abandonment, expansion, flexibility) increase project NPV: $\text{Project NPV} = \text{Static NPV} + \text{Option Value}$.

Worked Numerical 1: Project NPV, IRR & Profitability Index

An industrial robotics upgrade requires 500,000 USD upfront ($t=0$) and returns 180,000 USD ($t=1$), 220,000 USD ($t=2$), and 250,000 USD ($t=3$) at WACC $= 10.0\%$. $NPV = 33,283.25\text{ USD}$, $IRR = 13.54\%$, and $PI = 1.067$.

03. Working Capital, Liquidity & Short-Term Financing

Cash Conversion Cycle & Effective Annual Cost of Trade Credit
$$CCC = DOH + DSO - DPO, \qquad EAR_{\text{trade credit}} = \left(1 + \frac{\%\text{Discount}}{100 - \%\text{Discount}}\right)^{\frac{365}{\text{Net Days} - \text{Discount Days}}} - 1$$

Worked Numerical 2: Cash Conversion Cycle & Foregoing '2/10, Net 30' Trade Discount

CCC: With $DOH = 52$, $DSO = 44$, and $DPO = 38$, $CCC = 52 + 44 - 38 = 58\text{ days}$ (operating cycle $= 52 + 44 = 96$ days).

Trade Credit Cost: Supplier offers terms '2/10, net 30'. Missing the 2% discount to borrow for 20 extra days costs an annualized $EAR = \left(1 + \frac{2}{98}\right)^{365/20} - 1 = 44.59\%$—far costlier than a 7% bank line!

04. Cost of Capital, Flotation Costs & Pure-Play Hamada Beta

WACC & Pure-Play Beta Unlevering / Relevering
$$WACC = w_d r_d(1-t) + w_p r_p + w_e r_e, \qquad r_p = \frac{D_p}{P_p}$$ $$\beta_U = \frac{\beta_L}{1 + (1 - t)\left(\frac{D}{E}\right)_{\text{comp}}}, \qquad \beta_{\text{proj}} = \beta_U \left[1 + (1 - t_{\text{proj}})\left(\frac{D}{E}\right)_{\text{proj}}\right]$$

Exam Trap on Flotation Costs: NEVER add flotation costs into WACC permanently! Correct practice adjusts initial cash outflow $CF_0$ upward by dollar flotation cost (or divides $NPV$ adjustment cleanly at $t=0$).

Worked Numerical 3: Pure-Play Hamada Beta & Divisional Project WACC

A pure-play peer has $\beta_L = 1.25$, $D/E = 0.60$, tax rate $25\%$. Unlevered asset beta $\beta_U = \frac{1.25}{1 + 0.75(0.60)} = 0.8621$. Relevering for our division ($D/E = 0.80$, $t = 30\%$): $\beta_{\text{proj}} = 0.8621[1 + 0.70(0.80)] = 1.3448$. With $R_f = 4.0\%$, $ERP = 5.5\%$, pre-tax $r_d = 6.5\%$: $r_e = 11.40\%$, and $WACC = (0.8/1.8)(6.5\%)(0.70) + (1.0/1.8)(11.40\%) = 8.35\%$.

05. Measures of Leverage & Break-Even Points (DOL, DFL, DTL)

Operating, Financial & Total Leverage
$$DOL = \frac{Q(P - V)}{Q(P - V) - F}, \qquad DFL = \frac{EBIT}{EBIT - I}, \qquad DTL = DOL \times DFL = \frac{Q(P - V)}{Q(P - V) - F - I}$$ $$Q_{BE} = \frac{F + I}{P - V}, \qquad Q_{OBE} = \frac{F}{P - V}$$

Worked Numerical 4: Computing DOL, DFL, DTL & Break-Even Units

Selling $Q = 10,000$ units at $P = 80\text{ USD}$, variable cost $V = 45\text{ USD}$, fixed operating costs $F = 200,000\text{ USD}$, interest $I = 50,000\text{ USD}$. Contribution margin $= 350,000\text{ USD}$, $EBIT = 150,000\text{ USD}$. $DOL = 2.333$, $DFL = 1.500$, $DTL = 3.500$. Operating break-even $Q_{OBE} = 7,143$ units; Total break-even $Q_{BE} = 5,714$ units.

06. Capital Structure Theory: MM, Static Trade-Off & Pecking Order (L2)

Theory / WorldFirm Value ($V_L$)Cost of Equity ($r_e$)Optimal Debt Ratio
MM No Taxes$V_L = V_U$ (Irrelevant)$r_e = r_0 + (r_0 - r_d)(D/E)$No optimal ratio; WACC flat
MM With Corp Taxes$V_L = V_U + t \cdot D$$r_e = r_0 + (r_0 - r_d)(1-t)(D/E)$100% debt (ignoring distress)
Static Trade-Off$V_L = V_U + t D - PV(\text{Distress})$U-shaped WACCWhere $PV(\text{Marginal Tax Shield}) = PV(\text{Marginal Distress})$

Pecking Order Theory (Myers-Majluf): Due to asymmetric information, managers prefer financing in order: (1) Internal Retained Earnings → (2) Debt → (3) External Equity (issuing stock signals overvaluation!). Free Cash Flow hypothesis: debt disciplines empire-building managers.

Worked Numerical 5: Modigliani-Miller With Corporate Taxes

An all-equity firm earns perpetual pre-tax EBIT of 1,600,000 USD ($NI = 1,200,000\text{ USD}$ at $t=25\%$) with unlevered $r_0 = 12.0\%$, so $V_U = 10,000,000\text{ USD}$. It issues 4,000,000 USD permanent debt at $r_d = 6.0\%$ to buy back shares. $V_L = 10,000,000 + 0.25(4,000,000) = 11,000,000\text{ USD}$, $E = 7,000,000\text{ USD}$, and levered $r_e = 12\% + (12\% - 6\%)(0.75)(4/7) = 14.57\%$.

07. Dividends, Share Repurchases & Payout Policy (L2)

Target Payout Adjustment (Lintner) & Double-Taxation Effective Tax Rate
$$\Delta D_t = (\text{Target Payout} \times E_t - D_{t-1}) \times \text{Adjustment Factor}$$ $$\text{Effective Tax Rate on Dividends} = 1 - (1 - t_{\text{corp}})(1 - t_{\text{div}})$$
  • Buyback Impact on EPS: Accretive if Earnings Yield ($E/P$) > After-Tax Borrowing Cost (or After-Tax Cash Yield)!
  • Buyback Impact on BVPS: Increases Book Value Per Share ONLY if Repurchase Price < Pre-buyback BVPS!

Worked Numerical 6: Debt-Financed Share Repurchase EPS Accretion Test

A firm earns 24.0M USD across 8.0M shares ($EPS = 3.00\text{ USD}$) at $P = 30.00\text{ USD}$ ($E/P = 10.0\%$). It borrows 30.0M USD at 4.50% after-tax interest to repurchase 1.0M shares. Since $E/P (10.0\%) > r_d(1-t) (4.5\%)$, post-buyback $EPS = \frac{24.0\text{M} - 1.35\text{M}}{7.0\text{M}} = 3.24\text{ USD}$ (Accretive!).

08. Corporate Actions, Combinations & M&A Valuation (L2)

Merger Gain Split, Post-Merger EPS & Maximum Exchange Ratio
$$V_{AT} = V_A + V_T + S - C, \qquad \text{Gain}_T = P_T - V_T, \qquad \text{Gain}_A = S - \text{Premium}_T$$ $$\text{Stock Offer Exchange Ratio} = \frac{\text{Offer Price}_T}{\text{Acquirer Share Price}_A}$$
  • Takeover Defenses: Pre-offer (Poison Pills, Staggered Board, Supermajority, Golden Parachutes) vs. Post-offer (Pac-Man Defense, White Knight, Crown Jewel Defense, Greenmail).
  • Divestitures: Spin-off (new entity distributed pro-rata to existing shareholders, tax-free, no cash raised); Equity Carve-out (IPO of minority stake, raises cash); Split-off (shareholders swap parent shares for subsidiary shares).

Worked Numerical 7: Stock-for-Stock M&A Exchange Ratio & Post-Merger EPS

Acquirer ($50\text{M}$ shares, $EPS = 4.00\text{ USD}$, $P = 60.00\text{ USD}$) acquires Target ($10\text{M}$ shares, $EPS = 2.50\text{ USD}$) in a stock swap at 45.00 USD/share, generating 18.0M USD after-tax annual synergies. Exchange ratio $= 45 / 60 = 0.75$, issuing $10\text{M} \times 0.75 = 7.5\text{M}$ new shares. Post-merger $EPS = \frac{200\text{M} + 25\text{M} + 18\text{M}}{57.5\text{M}} = 4.23\text{ USD}$ (+0.23 USD accretive!).
