01. Market Forces of Supply, Demand & Elasticities

Price, Income & Cross-Price Elasticities
$$E_p^d = \frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta P} = \left(\frac{\Delta Q_d}{\Delta P}\right)\frac{P_0}{Q_0}, \qquad E_{arc} = \frac{(Q_2 - Q_1)/[(Q_1+Q_2)/2]}{(P_2 - P_1)/[(P_1+P_2)/2]}$$ $$E_I = \frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta I} \; (>0 \text{ Normal}, <0 \text{ Inferior}), \qquad E_{XY} = \frac{\%\Delta Q_X}{\%\Delta P_Y} \; (>0 \text{ Substitutes}, <0 \text{ Complements})$$

Total Revenue Test: When $|E_p^d| > 1$ (elastic), raising price decreases total revenue; when $|E_p^d| < 1$ (inelastic), raising price increases total revenue. Giffen goods (inferior + strong negative income effect > substitution effect) exhibit upward-sloping demand; Veblen goods reflect prestige consumption.

Worked Numerical 1: Arc Price Elasticity & Total Revenue Effect

Raising software subscription price from 50 USD to 55 USD reduces users from 1,000 to 850. Arc elasticity $E_{arc} = \frac{-150 / 925}{5 / 52.5} = -1.703$. Because $|E_{arc}| = 1.703 > 1$ (elastic), revenue falls from 50,000 USD to 46,750 USD.

03. Business Cycles, Inflation, Deflation & Economic Indicators

  • Four Phases: Expansion (capex rises, inventories lean) → Peak (bottlenecks, inflation rises) → Contraction/Recession (two quarters negative GDP; inventory-to-sales spikes initially then draws down) → Trough (hiring starts with temp workers before permanent payrolls!).
  • Indicator Classification: Leading (S&P 500, building permits, ISM new orders, yield spread, initial jobless claims inverted, average weekly manufacturing hours); Coincident (industrial production, nonfarm payrolls, personal income); Lagging (unemployment rate, prime rate, average duration of unemployment, CPI services).
  • Inflation Price Indices: Laspeyres (fixed base basket → overstates inflation via substitution/quality bias); Paasche (current basket → understates inflation); Fisher $= \sqrt{\text{Laspeyres} \times \text{Paasche}}$ (ideal chained index).
Module 03 Chapter Exercises (Cycles & Inflation)
Exercise 3.1 (Solved): Laspeyres vs. Paasche Inflation Bias
L1 Macro SOLVED WALKTHROUGH

Year 0 basket is 10 apples (1.00 USD) and 5 coffees (2.00 USD). In Year 1, apples rise to 1.50 USD and coffees remain 2.00 USD, so consumers buy 6 apples and 9 coffees. Compute Laspeyres and Paasche CPI (base = 100).

Solution & Step-by-Step Ruling (Expanded — Click to Collapse)

Answer: Laspeyres = 125.0; Paasche = 112.5. Laspeyres $= \frac{10(1.5)+5(2)}{10(1)+5(2)} \times 100 = 125.0$. Paasche $= \frac{6(1.5)+9(2)}{6(1)+9(2)} \times 100 = \frac{27}{24} \times 100 = 112.5$. Fisher $= \sqrt{125 \times 112.5} = 118.59$.

04. Monetary System, Monetary & Fiscal Policy

Multipliers, Neutral Rate, Fisher Effect & Taylor Rule
$$\text{Money Multiplier} = \frac{1}{\text{Reserve Req}}, \qquad \text{Fiscal Multiplier} = \frac{1}{1 - c(1 - t)}$$ $$R_{\text{nominal}} = r_{\text{real}} + E(\pi) + \text{IRP}, \qquad i^* = r_{\text{neutral}} + \pi + 0.5(\pi - \pi^*) + 0.5(y - y^*)$$
  • Ricardian Equivalence: Tax cuts financed by debt fail to boost aggregate demand if forward-looking households save the tax cut expecting future tax hikes.
  • Policy Mix Impact on Rates: Tight monetary + loose fiscal → sharply higher interest rates, stronger currency; Loose monetary + tight fiscal → lower interest rates.

Worked Numerical 2: Fiscal Multiplier & Taylor Rule Target Policy Rate

Fiscal Multiplier: Given $MPC = 0.80$ and tax rate $t = 25\%$, $\text{Multiplier} = \frac{1}{1 - 0.80(0.75)} = \frac{1}{0.40} = 2.50$. A 20B USD deficit-financed infrastructure spend raises GDP by $20\text{B} \times 2.50 = 50\text{B USD}$.

Taylor Rule: Neutral real rate $2.0\%$, current inflation $3.4\%$ (target $2.0\%$), output gap $-0.8\%$. $i^* = 2.0\% + 3.4\% + 0.5(1.4\%) + 0.5(-0.8\%) = 5.70\%$.

05. International Trade, Capital Flows & Geopolitics

  • Comparative Advantage (Ricardo): Gains from trade depend on lower opportunity cost, not absolute productivity! Heckscher-Ohlin attributes advantage to relative factor endowments (capital vs. labor).
  • Tariffs vs. Quotas: Both raise domestic price and produce deadweight loss (inefficiency + consumption loss). Tariffs yield government tax revenue; quotas transfer quota rents to foreign exporters unless licenses are auctioned!
  • Balance of Payments: $CA + KA + FA = 0$. Persistent current account deficits require capital/financial inflows ($S - I = X - M$: low domestic savings vs. investment drives current account deficits).
  • Geopolitics Archetypes: Autarky, Hegemony, Multilateralism, Bilateralism; evaluated via Velocity, Impact, and Likelihood.

06. FX Market Mechanics & Cross-Rate Calculations (L1 Module 8)

CFA Quote Convention ($\text{Price} / \text{Base}$) & Dealer Bid-Ask Cross Rates
$$A/B \text{ quote} = \text{Units of Price currency } A \text{ per 1 unit of Base currency } B$$ $$(A/C)_{\text{bid}} = (A/B)_{\text{bid}} \times (B/C)_{\text{bid}}, \qquad (A/C)_{\text{ask}} = (A/B)_{\text{ask}} \times (B/C)_{\text{ask}}$$ $$(B/A)_{\text{bid}} = \frac{1}{(A/B)_{\text{ask}}}, \qquad F_{P/B} = S_{P/B} \cdot \frac{1 + r_P (\text{Days}/360)}{1 + r_B (\text{Days}/360)}$$

Remember CFA Quote Notation: Unlike street convention in some desks, CFA reads $USD/EUR = 1.0800$ as 1.0800 USD per 1 EUR (Price = USD, Base = EUR)!

Worked Numerical 3: Dealer Bid-Ask Cross-Rate & 180-Day Forward Points

Cross Rate: Dealer quotes $USD/EUR = 1.0800\text{--}1.0810$ and $JPY/USD = 150.00\text{--}150.20$. Cross $JPY/EUR$: Bid $= 1.0800 \times 150.00 = 162.00$, Ask $= 1.0810 \times 150.20 = 162.3662$.

Forward Points: $S_{USD/GBP} = 1.2500$, $r_{USD} = 5.20\%$, $r_{GBP} = 3.80\%$, $T = 180$ days. $F = 1.2500 \times \frac{1 + 0.052(0.5)}{1 + 0.038(0.5)} = 1.2586 \implies \text{Forward Points} = +85.9\text{ pips}$.

07. Currency Exchange Rates: Parity Conditions & Carry Trade (L2 Module 2)

Parity ConditionGoverning Equation ($P/B$)Holds Short/Long Run?
Covered IRP (CIRP)$$F/S = (1+r_P)/(1+r_B)$$Yes — Enforced continuously by arbitrage!
Uncovered IRP (UIRP)$$E(\%\Delta S_{P/B}) \approx r_P - r_B$$Often fails short/med run → creates FX Carry Trade profits!
Relative PPP$$\%\Delta S_{P/B} \approx \pi_P - \pi_B$$Holds over long horizons (5–10+ yrs).
Intl Fisher Effect$$r_P - r_B \approx E(\pi_P) - E(\pi_B)$$Combines Fisher Effect + equal real rates.

Mundell-Fleming Model (Capital Mobility): Under high capital mobility + floating FX, expansionary Monetary policy lowers rates → capital outflow → currency depreciates → boosts exports (strong expansion!); under fixed FX, monetary policy is powerless and only Fiscal policy expands output. Dornbusch Overshooting: sticky goods prices force spot FX to overshoot long-run equilibrium immediately upon monetary shocks.

Worked Numerical 4: Unhedged FX Carry Trade Return

A hedge fund borrows JPY at 2.50% ($r_{\text{funding}}$) and invests in MXN deposits at 6.50% ($r_{\text{target}}$). Over 1 year, $JPY/MXN$ appreciates by +1.20% (MXN strengthens). Net return $= (1 + 0.065)(1.012) - (1 + 0.025) = 5.28\%$. Warning: Carry trades exhibit negative skewness / crash risk!

08. Economic Growth, Development & Regulation (L2 Module 1 & CBOK III)

Cobb-Douglas Growth Accounting & Solow Steady-State Equations
$$\frac{\Delta Y}{Y} = \frac{\Delta A}{A} + \alpha \frac{\Delta K}{K} + (1 - \alpha)\frac{\Delta L}{L}, \qquad g^* = \frac{\theta}{1 - \alpha} + n \; (\text{Steady-State Output Growth})$$ $$\text{Solow Steady-State Capital per Worker: } k^* = \left(\frac{s A}{\delta + n}\right)^{\frac{1}{1 - \alpha}}, \qquad \frac{I}{Y} = s = \left(\frac{\delta + n + \theta/(1-\alpha)}{Y/K}\right)\frac{K}{Y}$$
  • Neoclassical (Solow): Capital deepening alone yields diminishing marginal returns—only Total Factor Productivity (TFP $\theta$) drives permanent per-capita growth! Supports conditional convergence (poor nations grow faster given similar savings/institutions).
  • Endogenous Growth (Romer): R&D, human capital, and knowledge spillovers yield constant/increasing returns to capital—permanent growth without exogenous shocks.
  • Economics of Regulation: Regulatory capture occurs when regulators serve the regulated industry rather than the public; cost-benefit analysis weighs compliance burden against systemic stability.

Worked Numerical 5: Cobb-Douglas Potential GDP Growth & Solow Steady State

Growth Accounting: TFP growth $= 1.4\%$, capital share $\alpha = 0.35$, capital stock growth $= 3.2\%$, labor growth $= 1.0\%$. Potential GDP growth $= 1.4\% + 0.35(3.2\%) + 0.65(1.0\%) = 3.17\%$.

Solow Steady State: Savings rate $s=24\%$, $A=1.0$, $\alpha=0.40$, depreciation $\delta=6\%$, labor growth $n=2\%$. $k^* = \left(\frac{0.24}{0.08}\right)^{1/0.60} = 3.0^{1.6667} = 6.24$.
