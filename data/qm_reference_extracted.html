01. Rates, Returns & Benchmarking (2027 L1 Modules 1–3)

Interest rates serve simultaneously as required rates of return, discount rates, and opportunity costs: $r = r_{\text{real}} + E(\pi) + \text{Default} + \text{Liquidity} + \text{Maturity}$.

Effective Annual Rate (EAR), Geometric vs. Harmonic Mean & Real After-Tax Return
$$EAR = \left(1 + \frac{r_s}{m}\right)^m - 1, \qquad EAR_{\text{cont}} = e^{r_s} - 1$$ $$\bar{R}_G = \left[\prod_{t=1}^T (1 + R_t)\right]^{1/T} - 1, \qquad \bar{X}_H = \frac{n}{\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{1}{X_i}}, \qquad \bar{X}_H \le \bar{R}_G \le \bar{R}_A$$ $$R_{\text{after-tax, real}} = \frac{1 + R_{\text{nominal}}(1 - t)}{1 + \pi} - 1$$

Exam Traps: Use the harmonic mean when averaging purchase prices under constant dollar cost-averaging or aggregating valuation multiples like P/E across a portfolio. Use trimmed/winsorized means to handle outliers.

Worked Numerical 1: Compounding EAR, Geometric Mean & Harmonic Dollar-Cost Averaging

Part A (EAR): An 8.00% stated annual rate compounded monthly yields $EAR = (1 + 0.08/12)^{12} - 1 = 8.300\%$; compounded continuously yields $e^{0.08} - 1 = 8.329\%$.

Part B (Geometric vs. Arithmetic): Annual returns of +14.0%, -8.0%, +19.0% give Arithmetic Mean $= 8.33\%$ and Geometric Mean $= [(1.14)(0.92)(1.19)]^{1/3} - 1 = 7.67\%$.

Part C (Harmonic Mean): Investing 1,000 USD quarterly at 42 USD, 48 USD, and 55 USD gives average cost per share $= \frac{3}{1/42 + 1/48 + 1/55} = 47.75\text{ USD}$.

02. Time Value of Money in Finance (2027 L1 Module 4)

The 2027 L1 syllabus integrates TVM directly into bond discounting, perpetual preferred/equity valuation, implied growth rates, and the Cash Flow Additivity Principle (two cash flow streams with identical future payoffs in all states must have identical present values—forming the basis of no-arbitrage forward pricing).

Annuities, Perpetuities & Forward Rate Implication
$$PV_{\text{ord}} = PMT \cdot \frac{1 - (1+r)^{-N}}{r}, \qquad PV_{\text{due}} = PV_{\text{ord}} \cdot (1+r), \qquad PV_{\text{perp}} = \frac{PMT}{r - g}$$ $$(1 + z_2)^2 = (1 + z_1)(1 + f_{1,1}) \implies f_{1,1} = \frac{(1+z_2)^2}{1+z_1} - 1$$

Worked Numerical 2: Cash Flow Additivity & Implied 1-Year Forward Rate

If the 1-year spot rate is $z_1 = 4.60\%$ and the 2-year spot rate is $z_2 = 5.40\%$, cash flow additivity prevents arbitrage only if the 1-year forward rate one year from today equals: $f_{1,1} = \frac{1.054^2}{1.046} - 1 = 6.21\%$.

04. Probability, Bayes' Theorem & Distributions (2027 L1 Module 6)

Bayes' Updating, Roy's Safety-First Criterion & Lognormal Pricing
$$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(B \mid A) P(A)}{P(B)}, \qquad \text{SFRatio} = \frac{E(R_p) - R_L}{\sigma_p}$$
  • Normal vs. Lognormal: Continuously compounded asset returns follow a Normal distribution; asset prices (bounded below by zero, positively skewed) follow a Lognormal distribution.
  • Monte Carlo vs. Parametric Normal: Student's t ($df < 30$) captures fatter tails; Chi-Square and F-distributions govern variance tests (always right-skewed, non-negative).

Worked Numerical 3: Bayes' Theorem & Roy's Safety-First Portfolio Selection

Bayes: Prior probability of an earnings beat is $P(\text{Beat}) = 40\%$. Historically, 80% of beating firms rally ($P(\text{Up}\mid\text{Beat})=0.80$), while 25% of missing firms rally ($P(\text{Up}\mid\text{Miss})=0.25$). Given a stock rallies today, updated probability of a beat is: $P(\text{Beat}\mid\text{Up}) = \frac{0.40 \times 0.80}{0.40(0.80)+0.60(0.25)} = \frac{0.32}{0.47} = 68.09\%$.

Roy's Safety-First ($R_L = 3.0\%$): Fund A ($E=11\%, \sigma=16\%$) gives $SFR_A = 0.500$; Fund B ($E=14\%, \sigma=24\%$) gives $SFR_B = 0.458$. Fund A maximizes safety-first utility.

05. Sampling, Estimation, Biases & Hypothesis Testing (2027 L1 Module 7)

Test Statistics & Parametric vs. Nonparametric Selection
$$t = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu_0}{s / \sqrt{n}} \; (df=n-1), \qquad \chi^2 = \frac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma_0^2}, \qquad F = \frac{s_1^2}{s_2^2} \; (s_1^2 \ge s_2^2)$$
  • Biases: Data snooping / p-hacking (testing dozens of strategies until one hits $p<0.05$), Survivorship bias (omitting delisted funds), Look-ahead bias (using accounting data before its filing date), Time-period bias.
  • Errors & Power: Type I ($lpha = P(\text{Reject true } H_0)$); Type II ($eta = P(\text{Fail to reject false } H_0)$); Power $= 1 - eta$.
  • Nonparametric Equivalents: Wilcoxon signed-rank (paired), Mann-Whitney U (independent), Spearman rank correlation (ordinal/outliers).

Worked Numerical 4: One-Sample t-Test on Active Alpha

Over $n=36$ months, a fund earns mean monthly excess return $\bar{X} = +1.20\%$ with sample SD $s = 3.60\%$. Test $H_0: \mu = 0$: $t = \frac{0.012 - 0}{0.036 / \sqrt{36}} = \frac{0.012}{0.006} = 2.00$. Since $t = 2.00$ vs. two-tailed $t_{0.025, 35} \approx 2.03$, we fail to reject $H_0$ at $\alpha = 5\%$.

06. Simple, Multiple & Logistic Regression (L1 Module 10 & L2 Modules 1–2, 4)

OLS ANOVA, Adjusted $R^2$, F-Test & Logistic Probability
$$\hat{b}_1 = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\text{Var}(X)}, \qquad R^2 = \frac{\text{SSR}}{\text{SST}}, \qquad \bar{R}^2 = 1 - \left(\frac{n-1}{n-k-1}\right)(1 - R^2), \qquad F = \frac{\text{MSR}}{\text{MSE}} = \frac{\text{SSR}/k}{\text{SSE}/(n-k-1)}$$ $$\ln\left(\frac{P}{1-P}\right) = b_0 + b_1 X_1 + \dots + b_k X_k \implies P(Y=1) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\text{log-odds}}}$$

Key L2 Metrics: Use AIC (prediction accuracy) or BIC (parsimony, stricter penalty for $k$) to compare nested/non-nested models—lower is better. Watch for high-leverage points ($h_{ii} > 3(k+1)/n$) and Cook's $D > \sqrt{k/n}$.

Worked Numerical 5: OLS Regression Slope & Logistic Default Probability

Simple OLS: If $\text{Cov}(R_i, R_m) = 0.0288$ and $\text{Var}(R_m) = 0.0256$, slope $\beta = \frac{0.0288}{0.0256} = 1.125$.

Logistic Regression (L2): Log-odds of bond default $= -1.25 + 0.85(\text{Debt/EBITDA}) - 0.40(\text{Interest Cov})$. For Debt/EBITDA $= 2.0$ and Coverage $= 1.5$, log-odds $= -0.150$, giving $P(\text{Default}) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(-0.150)}} = 46.26\%$.

08. Time-Series Forecasting & Volatility Models (L2 Module 5 & CBOK II)

AR(1) Mean Reversion, Random Walk, Dickey-Fuller & GARCH(1,1)
$$X_t = b_0 + b_1 X_{t-1} + \epsilon_t \implies \bar{X} = \frac{b_0}{1 - b_1} \; (|b_1| < 1), \qquad \text{Random Walk: } b_1 = 1 \; (\text{Unit Root})$$ $$\text{Seasonality AR(1): } X_t = b_0 + b_1 X_{t-1} + b_2 X_{t-4} + \epsilon_t$$ $$\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \alpha \epsilon_{t-1}^2 + \beta \sigma_{t-1}^2, \qquad \text{Long-Run Variance } V_L = \frac{\omega}{1 - \alpha - \beta}$$
  • Covariance Stationarity: Constant finite mean, constant finite variance, and constant covariance across lags. Never use standard DW on an AR model—inspect residual autocorrelations via $t$-stats!
  • Cointegration: Two unit-root non-stationary series are cointegrated if a linear combination (error term) is stationary (tested via Engle-Granger DF test)—enabling pairs trading and valid long-run regressions.

Worked Numerical 6: AR(1) Mean Reversion & GARCH(1,1) Volatility Forecast

AR(1): Monthly inflation follows $X_t = 0.018 + 0.64 X_{t-1}$. Mean-reverting level $= \frac{0.018}{1 - 0.64} = 5.00\%$. Given $X_t = 6.50\%$, $\hat{X}_{t+1} = 5.960\%$ and $\hat{X}_{t+2} = 5.614\%$.

GARCH(1,1): Parameters $\omega = 0.000012$, $\alpha = 0.10$, $\beta = 0.85$. Yesterday's shock $\epsilon_{t-1} = +2.50\%$ and prior volatility $\sigma_{t-1} = 1.80\%$. Today's variance $\sigma_t^2 = 0.000012 + 0.10(0.025)^2 + 0.85(0.018)^2 = 0.000350 \implies \sigma_t = 1.87\%$.

09. Simulation, Backtesting & Model Governance (2027 L1 Module 9 & CBOK II)

  • Monte Carlo Simulation: Generates thousands of correlated stochastic paths via parametric distributions (Cholesky decomposition for correlated factors) — ideal for path-dependent options and multi-period retirement planning.
  • Historical Simulation & Bootstrapping: Bootstrapping resamples with replacement from historical returns, preserving empirical fat tails and skewness without imposing normality.
  • Model Governance & Walk-Forward Backtesting: Prevents look-ahead/survivorship bias via expanding/rolling out-of-sample windows, sensitivity sweeps, hyperparameter freeze before out-of-sample test, and ongoing drift/PSI monitoring.

10. Portfolio Return & Risk Mathematics (2027 L1 Module 8)

Two-Asset & Three-Asset Portfolio Variance
$$\sigma_p^2 = w_1^2\sigma_1^2 + w_2^2\sigma_2^2 + 2w_1w_2\text{Cov}(R_1,R_2) = w_1^2\sigma_1^2 + w_2^2\sigma_2^2 + 2w_1w_2\rho_{1,2}\sigma_1\sigma_2$$ $$\text{Minimum-Variance Weight (2 assets): } w_1^* = \frac{\sigma_2^2 - \text{Cov}(R_1,R_2)}{\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 - 2\text{Cov}(R_1,R_2)}$$

Worked Numerical 7: Two-Asset Portfolio Standard Deviation

Asset 1 ($w_1=60\%, \sigma_1=18\%$) and Asset 2 ($w_2=40\%, \sigma_2=12\%$) have correlation $\rho_{1,2} = +0.25$. $\sigma_p^2 = (0.36)(0.0324) + (0.16)(0.0144) + 2(0.6)(0.4)(0.25)(0.18)(0.12) = 0.016560 \implies \sigma_p = 12.87\%$ (vs. weighted average $15.60\%$, proving $2.76\%$ diversification gain).
