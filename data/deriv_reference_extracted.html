01. Derivative Instruments, Markets & No-Arbitrage Replication

  • Forward Commitments (Obligation to transact): Forwards (OTC, bespoke, counterparty default risk), Futures (exchange-traded, daily mark-to-market via CCP clearinghouse), Swaps (series of forwards).
  • Contingent Claims (Right, not obligation): Options (Calls/Puts), Credit Default Swaps (CDS).
  • No-Arbitrage Replication: $\text{Risky Asset} + \text{Short Forward} = \text{Risk-Free Bond}$. If futures prices correlate positively with interest rates, futures prices exceed forward prices (daily MTM cash inflows can be reinvested at higher rates!).

When underlying bond/equity asset prices correlate negatively with short-term interest rates, which has the higher price: a forward or a futures contract?

02. Pricing & Valuation of Forward Commitments (L1 & L2)

No-Arbitrage Forward Price & Mid-Life Valuation ($0 < t < T$)
$$F_0(T) = [S_0 - PV_0(\text{Benefits}) + PV_0(\text{Costs})](1 + r)^T, \qquad F_0^{\text{cont}} = S_0 e^{(r + c - \gamma)T}$$ $$V_t(\text{Long Forward}) = [S_t - PV_t(\text{Remaining Benefits}) + PV_t(\text{Remaining Costs})] - \frac{F_0(T)}{(1+r)^{T-t}}$$ $$\text{Bond Futures Quoted Price: } QFP = \frac{F_0(T) - AI_T}{CF}, \qquad F_0(\text{FX, } P/B) = S_0 \frac{(1+r_P)^T}{(1+r_B)^T}$$

Worked Numerical 1: Equity Forward Price & Mid-Life MTM Valuation

At $t=0$: Stock $S_0 = 120.00\text{ USD}$ pays a 2.50 USD dividend in 0.25 years; $r_f = 4.0\%$, $T = 0.50$ years. $PV_0(D) = 2.50 / 1.04^{0.25} = 2.4756\text{ USD}$. Forward price $F_0 = (120 - 2.4756)(1.04)^{0.5} = 119.85\text{ USD}$.

At $t=0.30$ (after ex-dividend, $T-t = 0.20$): Stock trades at $S_t = 126.00\text{ USD}$. Long forward value $V_t = 126.00 - \frac{119.85}{1.04^{0.20}} = 7.08\text{ USD}$.

Worked Numerical 2: Commodity Forward with Storage & Convenience Yield

Oil spot $S_0 = 80.00\text{ USD}$, continuous $r_f = 4.0\%$, storage cost $c = 3.0\%$, convenience yield $\gamma = 5.0\%$, $T = 0.5$ yr. $F_0 = 80 e^{(0.04 + 0.03 - 0.05)(0.5)} = 80.80\text{ USD}$.

03. Forward Rate Agreements (FRAs) & Interest Rate Swaps (L2)

FRA Synthetic Rate, FRA Valuation & Swap Fixed Rate
$$FRA(0, h, m) = \left[\frac{1 + L_{h+m}(\frac{h+m}{360})}{1 + L_h(\frac{h}{360})} - 1\right]\left(\frac{360}{m}\right)$$ $$V_t(\text{Pay-Fixed FRA}) = \text{NP} \cdot \frac{(FRA_t - FRA_0)(\frac{m}{360})}{1 + L_{T-t}(\frac{T-t}{360})}$$ $$r_{\text{fix, period}} = \frac{1 - DF_n}{\sum_{i=1}^n DF_i}, \qquad V_t(\text{Receive-Fixed Swap}) = NP \cdot (r_{\text{fix},0} - r_{\text{fix},t}) \sum DF_i \text{ (or Fixed Bond - Floating Bond)}$$

Worked Numerical 3: 3x6 FRA Rate & Mid-Life Pay-Fixed FRA Valuation

FRA Pricing: 90-day spot $L_{90} = 4.00\%$, 180-day spot $L_{180} = 4.50\%$. Implied 3x6 FRA $= 4.950\%$.

Mid-Life FRA Value: Long (pay-fixed) a 10M USD FRA locked at $4.20\%$ on 90-day MRR. 60 days later ($150$ days to final settlement), current forward 90-day rate is $5.10\%$ and 150-day discount rate is $5.00\%$. $V_t = 10\text{M} \times \frac{(0.051 - 0.042)(90/360)}{1 + 0.050(150/360)} = +22,040.82\text{ USD}$.

Worked Numerical 4: Bootstrapping Fixed Swap Rate from Discount Factors

Quarterly discount factors: $DF_{90}=0.9901, DF_{180}=0.9794, DF_{270}=0.9675, DF_{360}=0.9550$. Quarterly fixed rate $= \frac{1 - 0.9550}{3.8920} = 1.1562\% \implies$ Annualized Swap Fixed Rate $= 4.625\%$.

04. Currency Swaps & Equity Swaps (L2)

  • Currency Swaps: Notional principals in two currencies ARE exchanged at initiation and re-exchanged at maturity! Mid-life MTM value equals $\text{PV}(\text{Receive Bond}) - S_t \cdot \text{PV}(\text{Pay Bond})$ converted at current spot FX $S_t$!
  • Equity Swaps: Value of receive-equity / pay-fixed swap on date $t$ equals $NP \cdot \frac{S_t}{S_{t-1}} - \text{PV}(\text{Remaining Fixed Coupons + Notional})$ (remember: if equity index drops since last reset, the equity receiver must PAY the negative return PLUS the fixed/floating leg!).

05. Option Mechanics, Payoffs & Put-Call Parity (L1 & L2)

Put-Call Parity & Put-Call-Forward Parity
$$c_0 + \frac{X}{(1+r)^T} = S_0 + p_0 \quad (\text{Fiduciary Call = Protective Put})$$ $$c_0 + \frac{X - F_0(T)}{(1+r)^T} = p_0 \quad (\text{Put-Call-Forward Parity})$$
  • Option Value Bounds: European Call $\ge \max(0, S_0 - X/(1+r)^T)$; European Put $\ge \max(0, X/(1+r)^T - S_0)$. American puts may be exercised early when deep in-the-money ($r > 0$); American calls on non-dividend stocks are NEVER optimal to exercise early!

Worked Numerical 5: Put-Call Parity & Synthetic Put Arbitrage

Stock $S_0 = 52.00\text{ USD}$, $X = 50.00\text{ USD}$, $r_f = 5.0\%$, $T = 0.50$ yr, European call $c_0 = 4.80\text{ USD}$. Fair no-arbitrage European put price $p_0 = 4.80 + \frac{50.00}{1.05^{0.5}} - 52.00 = 1.60\text{ USD}$. If market put trades at 2.10 USD (overpriced), sell the market put and buy synthetic put ($+c_0 - S_0 + PV(X)$) for riskless profit!

06. Binomial Option Pricing Model (L1 & L2)

Risk-Neutral Probability, Hedge Ratio & Backward Induction
$$\pi_u = \frac{(1+r) - d}{u - d}, \qquad c_0 = \frac{\pi_u c_u + (1 - \pi_u) c_d}{1 + r}, \qquad \Delta = \frac{c_u - c_d}{S_u - S_d}$$ $$\text{American Put Node Check: } V_t = \max\!\left(X - S_t, \; \frac{\pi_u V_{u} + (1 - \pi_u) V_{d}}{1 + r}\right)$$

Crucial L2 Insight: Real-world directional probabilities ($P$) NEVER enter the option valuation formula—only risk-neutral probabilities ($\pi_u$) determined by no-arbitrage replication!

Worked Numerical 6: 1-Period Binomial Call & Replication Hedge Ratio

Spot $S_0 = 100\text{ USD}$, $X = 102\text{ USD}$, $u = 1.15$ ($S_u = 115$), $d = 0.88$ ($S_d = 88$), $r = 4.0\%$. Risk-neutral $\pi_u = \frac{1.04 - 0.88}{1.15 - 0.88} = 0.5926$. Payoffs: $c_u = 13$, $c_d = 0$. $c_0 = \frac{0.5926 \times 13}{1.04} = 7.41\text{ USD}$, with hedge ratio $\Delta = \frac{13 - 0}{115 - 88} = 0.4815$.

07. Black-Scholes-Merton (BSM) Model & Option Greeks (L2)

BSM Model with Dividend Yield $q$, Black Futures Model & Greeks
$$c = S_0 e^{-qT} N(d_1) - X e^{-rT} N(d_2), \qquad p = X e^{-rT} N(-d_2) - S_0 e^{-qT} N(-d_1)$$ $$d_1 = \frac{\ln(S_0/X) + (r - q + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2)T}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}, \qquad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T}$$ $$\Delta_c = e^{-qT}N(d_1), \quad \Delta_p = e^{-qT}[N(d_1)-1], \quad \Delta\text{ Portfolio Hedge Shares} = -\frac{N_{\text{opt}} \cdot \Delta_{\text{opt}}}{1}, \quad \Delta c \approx \Delta \cdot \Delta S + \frac{1}{2}\Gamma(\Delta S)^2$$
  • BSM Assumptions: Geometric Brownian motion (lognormal prices), constant known volatility $\sigma$ and risk-free rate $r$, continuous trading, no taxes/costs, European exercise.
  • Black Model (Futures/Interest Rate Options): Replaces $S_0 e^{-qT}$ with $F_0(T) e^{-rT}$!
  • Gamma vs. Theta Trade-off: Long ATM options near expiration have peak positive Gamma ($\Gamma$) and peak negative Theta ($\Theta$) (rapid time decay). Dynamic delta-neutral hedging profits when realized volatility exceeds implied volatility!

Worked Numerical 7: Full Python-Exact Black-Scholes-Merton & Delta/Gamma Hedge

Inputs: $S_0 = 100\text{ USD}$, $K = 100\text{ USD}$, $r = 5.0\%$, $q = 2.0\%$, $T = 0.50\text{ yr}$, $\sigma = 24.0\%$.

  • $d_1 = 0.1732 \implies N(d_1) = 0.5688$; $d_2 = 0.0035 \implies N(d_2) = 0.5014$.
  • Call Price $= 7.41\text{ USD}$; Put Price $= 5.93\text{ USD}$.
  • Greeks: Call $\Delta_c = +0.5631$, Put $\Delta_p = -0.4269$, $\Gamma = 0.0229$, Vega $= 27.51$ per $100\%$ vol (or $0.275$ per $1\%$ vol).
