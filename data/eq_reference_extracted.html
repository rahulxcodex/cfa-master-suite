01. Market Organization, Security Trading & Indexes

Financial markets channel capital from savers to productive investment. Analysts must evaluate market order execution mechanisms, leverage trading limits, and benchmark index construction.

Margin Transactions & Margin Call Pricing

When buying securities on margin, the investor borrows part of the purchase price from the broker. The Margin Call Price ($P$) is the stock price below which the broker requires additional equity collateral:

Margin Call Trigger Price Formula
$$P = P_0 \times \left( \frac{1 - \text{Initial Margin}}{1 - \text{Maintenance Margin}} \right)$$ $$\text{Where: } P_0 = \text{Initial purchase price}, \quad IM = \text{Initial equity margin %}, \quad MM = \text{Maintenance margin %}$$

Equity Index Construction Methodologies

Weighting Method Weighting Basis Stock Split Impact Prominent Example
Price-Weighted Price per share divided by index divisor: $w_i = P_i / \sum P_k$. Requires divisor adjustment; high-priced stocks have disproportionate influence. Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA), Nikkei 225.
Value-Weighted (Market-Cap) Total market capitalization: $w_i = (P_i Q_i) / \sum (P_k Q_k)$. Often float-adjusted. No divisor adjustment required for stock splits; mirrors true aggregate wealth. S&P 500, MSCI World, FTSE 100.
Equal-Weighted Each constituent stock receives identical weight: $w_i = 1 / N$. Requires frequent rebalancing as stock prices diverge; small-cap tilt. S&P 500 Equal Weight Index.

Shorting Equity Securities, Voting Jurisdictions & Market Efficiency (CBOK VI & 2027 L1)

  • Shorting Equities & Short Margin Call: A short seller borrows shares through a broker, sells them at $P_0$, deposits initial margin ($IM$), and remains liable for dividends paid during the short period plus any stock loan fee. The broker pays interest on cash proceeds minus the loan fee (the short rebate rate; hard-to-borrow stocks carry negative rebate rates and risk a short squeeze). Trigger price for a short margin call: $$P_{\text{Short Call}} = P_0 \times \left(\frac{1 + IM}{1 + MM}\right)$$
  • Equity Features, Jurisdictions & Depositary Receipts (2027 L1 LM1–LM2): Statutory voting assigns one vote per share per board seat; cumulative voting lets minority shareholders concentrate votes ($\text{shares} \times \text{seats}$) onto a single director. Dual-class shares grant superior voting rights to founders. ADRs (Level I OTC, Level II listed, Level III capital-raising, Rule 144A private) vs. GDRs trade foreign equities across borders. Preference shares may be cumulative, participating, callable, or convertible.
  • Market Efficiency & Anomalies (EMH): Weak-form rejects technical analysis; Semi-strong-form rejects both technical and fundamental analysis of public data; Strong-form prices reflect even private insider information. Apparent calendar, size, value, or momentum anomalies often stem from unmodeled risk factors, behavioral biases, or data mining.

Worked Numerical Example 1: Long & Short Margin Call Triggers & Leveraged Return

Scenario: Initial margin $IM = 50\%$, maintenance margin $MM = 30\%$, initial stock price $P_0 = 80.00\text{ USD}$ on 1,000 shares.

  1. Long Position Margin Call Price: $P_{\text{Long Call}} = 80.00 \times \frac{1 - 0.50}{1 - 0.30} = 80.00 \times \frac{0.50}{0.70} = 57.14\text{ USD}$.
  2. Long Leveraged Return: If stock rises to 96.00 USD, pays 2.00 USD/share dividend, and margin loan interest on 40,000 USD is 6.0% ($2,400\text{ USD}$): $$R_{\text{Leveraged}} = \frac{1,000(96 - 80) + 1,000(2) - 2,400}{40,000} = \frac{15,600}{40,000} = 39.00\%$$
  3. Short Sale Margin Call Price: $P_{\text{Short Call}} = 80.00 \times \frac{1 + 0.50}{1 + 0.30} = 80.00 \times \frac{1.50}{1.30} = 92.31\text{ USD}$.

Worked Numerical Example 2: Price-Weighted, Market-Cap-Weighted & Equal-Weighted Index Returns

Scenario: A 3-stock index consists of Stock A ($P_0=100, Q=1\text{M} \to P_1=110$), Stock B ($P_0=50, Q=4\text{M} \to P_1=55$), and Stock C ($P_0=30, Q=10\text{M} \to P_1=27$).

  • Price-Weighted Return: $\text{Index}_0 = \frac{100+50+30}{3} = 60.00$, $\text{Index}_1 = \frac{110+55+27}{3} = 64.00 \implies R_{PW} = \frac{64}{60} - 1 = +6.67\%$. If Stock A executes a 2-for-1 split at $t=0$ ($P_A \to 50$), the new divisor is $d_{\text{new}} = \frac{50+50+30}{60.00} = 2.1667$.
  • Market-Cap Weighted Return: $MC_0 = 100 + 200 + 300 = 600\text{M}$, $MC_1 = 110 + 220 + 270 = 600\text{M} \implies R_{MCW} = 0.00\%$.
  • Equal-Weighted Return: $\frac{1}{3}(+10.0\% + 10.0\% - 10.0\%) = +3.33\%$.

02. Industry & Competitive Strategy Analysis

Industry structure drives fundamental return on invested capital (ROIC). Michael Porter's Five Forces framework analyzes the competitive intensity and sustainable pricing power of an industry.

Porter's Five Forces Framework

  • Threat of New Entrants: High barriers to entry (economies of scale, patents, high switching costs) protect economic profits.
  • Bargaining Power of Buyers: Concentrated buyers or standardized commodity products reduce pricing power.
  • Bargaining Power of Suppliers: Fragmented buyers dealing with few specialized suppliers face high input costs.
  • Threat of Substitute Products: Close substitutes cap pricing power and elastic demand.
  • Rivalry Among Existing Competitors: Intense price competition occurs in fragmented industries with high exit barriers and slow growth.

Industry Life Cycle Stages

  1. Embryonic: Slow growth, high startup costs, unproven customer demand.
  2. Growth: Rapidly rising demand, improving profitability, falling production costs.
  3. Shakeout: Growth slows, overcapacity develops, price wars force weaker firms out.
  4. Mature: Slower growth matching GDP, consolidated oligopoly, focus on efficiency.
  5. Decline: Negative demand growth due to technological substitution.

Financial Statement Forecasting, Research Reports & ESG Integration (2027 L1 & CBOK VI)

  • Disaggregated Financial Forecasting: Combine Top-Down drivers (GDP growth, market share) with Bottom-Up unit drivers (stores $\times$ sales/store, volume $\times$ ASP). Model COGS and SG&A separately via fixed vs. variable operating leverage, working capital via DSO/DIO/DPO days, and CapEx into maintenance vs. growth buckets.
  • Equity Analyst Research Reports: Initiation of Coverage reports cover company overview, industry Porter analysis, financial forecasts, valuation, investment risks, and ESG factors; Update Reports analyze quarterly earnings surprises and catalyst revisions. Sell-side reports serve external institutional/retail clients; buy-side research serves internal portfolio managers.
  • Sustainability / ESG in Equity Valuation: Material ESG risks translate into DCF inputs—adjusting revenue growth (green demand / carbon regulation), operating margins (transition costs), cost of capital (beta/credit spread), or terminal value (stranded assets).

Worked Numerical Example 3: Probability-Weighted Scenario Valuation

Scenario: Following disaggregated financial modeling across three macro/regulatory scenarios, an equity analyst estimates intrinsic equity values of 58.00 USD (Bull, 25% probability), 46.00 USD (Base, 50% probability), and 32.00 USD (Bear, 25% probability). Current market price is 40.00 USD.

$$E(V_0) = (0.25 \times 58.00) + (0.50 \times 46.00) + (0.25 \times 32.00) = 14.50 + 23.00 + 8.00 = 45.50\text{ USD}$$

Implied Upside: $\frac{45.50 - 40.00}{40.00} = +13.75\% \implies$ Buy / Outperform recommendation.

03. Dividend Discount Models & Multi-Stage Valuation

The Dividend Discount Model (DDM) values common stock as the present value of all expected future cash dividend payments discounted at the required return on equity ($r$).

Cost of Equity Estimation Models (2027 L1 LM12 & L2 Return Concepts)

CAPM, Blume Adjusted Beta, Expanded CAPM & Country Risk Premium
$$r_{\text{CAPM}} = R_f + \beta_{\text{adj}} \times ERP, \qquad \beta_{\text{adj}} = \frac{2}{3}\beta_{\text{raw}} + \frac{1}{3}(1.0)$$ $$r_{\text{Expanded CAPM}} = R_f + \beta_{\text{peer}}(ERP) + SP + IP + SCRP$$ $$CRP = \text{Sovereign Yield Spread} \times \left(\frac{\sigma_{\text{Equity}}}{\sigma_{\text{Bond}}}\right), \qquad r_{\text{BYPRP}} = \text{Bond Yield} + \text{Risk Premium}$$ $$\text{Fama-French 3-Factor + Liquidity: } r = R_f + \beta_{mkt}ERP + \beta_{size}SMB + \beta_{val}HML + \beta_{liq}LIQ$$

Worked Numerical Example 4: Adjusted Beta, Country Risk Premium & Expanded CAPM

Scenario: An emerging-market mid-cap stock has raw historical $\beta_{\text{raw}} = 1.30$, risk-free rate $R_f = 4.00\%$, developed ERP $= 5.00\%$, small-stock premium $SP = 1.50\%$, company-specific risk premium $SCRP = 1.00\%$, sovereign yield spread $= 1.80\%$, and equity-to-sovereign-bond volatility ratio $= 1.25$.

  1. Blume's Adjusted Beta: $\beta_{\text{adj}} = \frac{2}{3}(1.30) + \frac{1}{3}(1.00) = 1.20$.
  2. Country Risk Premium (CRP): $CRP = 1.80\% \times 1.25 = 2.25\%$.
  3. Required Return on Equity: $r = 4.00\% + 1.20(5.00\%) + 1.50\% + 1.00\% + 2.25\% = 14.75\%$.

Gordon Growth Model & Sustainable Growth Rate (PRAT)

Constant Growth DDM (Gordon Growth)
$$V_0 = \frac{D_1}{r - g} = \frac{D_0 (1 + g)}{r - g} \quad (\text{Condition: } r > g)$$ $$g = b \times ROE = \left(\frac{NI - Div}{NI}\right) \times \left(\frac{NI}{Sales}\right) \times \left(\frac{Sales}{Assets}\right) \times \left(\frac{Assets}{Equity}\right) \quad (\text{PRAT Model})$$

Present Value of Growth Opportunities (PVGO)

A firm's stock value can be decomposed into the value of its current no-growth earnings stream plus the net present value of profitable growth investments:

PVGO Decomposition
$$V_0 = \frac{E_1}{r} + PVGO \implies PVGO = V_0 - \frac{E_1}{r}$$

Worked Numerical Example 5: Gordon Growth, PRAT & PVGO Decomposition

Scenario: Expected next-year EPS $E_1 = 5.00\text{ USD}$, Dividend Payout Ratio $= 50\%$ ($b = 0.50$, $D_1 = 2.50\text{ USD}$), $ROE = 16.0\%$, and required equity return $r = 12.0\%$.

  • Sustainable Growth Rate: $g = 0.50 \times 16.0\% = 8.00\%$.
  • Gordon Growth Intrinsic Value: $V_0 = \frac{2.50}{0.12 - 0.08} = 62.50\text{ USD}$.
  • No-Growth Value & PVGO: $\frac{E_1}{r} = \frac{5.00}{0.12} = 41.67\text{ USD} \implies PVGO = 62.50 - 41.67 = 20.83\text{ USD}$ (33.3% of total stock value comes from future growth opportunities!).

The H-Model for Linearly Transitioning Growth

The H-Model values a firm experiencing high initial growth ($g_S$) that transitions linearly over half-life $H$ (years $= 2H$) to a sustainable long-term rate ($g_L$):

H-Model Valuation Equation
$$V_0 = \frac{D_0 (1 + g_L)}{r - g_L} + \frac{D_0 \times H \times (g_S - g_L)}{r - g_L}, \qquad r_{\text{implied}} = \left(\frac{D_0}{P_0}\right)[(1 + g_L) + H(g_S - g_L)] + g_L$$

Worked Numerical Example 6: H-Model Valuation & Implied Expected Return

Scenario: A firm just paid $D_0 = 2.00\text{ USD}$. Dividend growth starts at $g_S = 14.0\%$ and declines linearly over 8 years ($H = 4$) to $g_L = 4.0\%$. Required return $r = 10.0\%$.

  1. Intrinsic Value ($V_0$): $$V_0 = \frac{2.00(1.04) + 2.00(4)(0.14 - 0.04)}{0.10 - 0.04} = \frac{2.08 + 0.80}{0.06} = \frac{2.88}{0.06} = 48.00\text{ USD}$$
  2. Implied Expected Return if Market Price $P_0 = 52.00\text{ USD}$: $$r_{\text{implied}} = \frac{2.00}{52.00}(1.04 + 0.40) + 0.04 = \frac{2.88}{52.00} + 0.04 = 5.54\% + 4.00\% = 9.54\%$$

04. Free Cash Flow Valuation (FCFF & FCFE)

Free cash flow models are superior when firms do not pay dividends, dividend policy does not reflect underlying profitability, or an acquiring firm seeks control of total operational cash flow.

Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF) Derivations

FCFF Multi-Source Accounting Identities
$$\text{From Net Income: } FCFF = NI + NCC + \text{Interest}(1 - T) - FCInv - WCInv$$ $$\text{From CFO: } FCFF = CFO + \text{Interest}(1 - T) - FCInv$$ $$\text{From EBIT: } FCFF = EBIT(1 - T) + Dep - FCInv - WCInv$$ $$\text{From EBITDA: } FCFF = EBITDA(1 - T) + (Dep \times T) - FCInv - WCInv$$

Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE) Derivations

FCFE Formulas & Net Borrowing Integration
$$FCFE = FCFF - \text{Interest}(1 - T) + \text{Net Borrowing}$$ $$FCFE = NI + NCC - FCInv - WCInv + \text{Net Borrowing}$$ $$FCFE = CFO - FCInv + \text{Net Borrowing}$$ $$\text{Where: } \text{Net Borrowing} = \text{New Debt Issued} - \text{Principal Repayments}$$
Visual 2: Cash Flow Waterfall Bridge from Operational Profit to Free Cash Flow
Step-by-step conversion of EBITDA down to Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF) and Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE).

Worked Numerical Example 7: FCFF & FCFE Derivation and Firm Valuation

Scenario: A firm reports Net Income $= 120\text{M USD}$, Depreciation (NCC) $= 35\text{M USD}$, Interest Expense $= 20\text{M USD}$, Tax Rate $T = 25\%$, Capital Expenditures ($FCInv$) $= 50\text{M USD}$, Working Capital Investment ($WCInv$) $= 15\text{M USD}$, and Net Debt Borrowing $= +10\text{M USD}$. $WACC = 9.0\%$, perpetual growth $g = 4.0\%$, and market value of debt $= 400\text{M USD}$.

  1. Free Cash Flow to Firm ($FCFF_0$): $$FCFF_0 = 120 + 35 + 20(1 - 0.25) - 50 - 15 = 120 + 35 + 15 - 50 - 15 = 105.00\text{M USD}$$
  2. Free Cash Flow to Equity ($FCFE_0$): $$FCFE_0 = 105.00 - 20(1 - 0.25) + 10 = 105.00 - 15.00 + 10.00 = 100.00\text{M USD}$$
  3. Intrinsic Firm & Equity Value: $$V_{\text{Firm}} = \frac{105.00(1.04)}{0.09 - 0.04} = \frac{109.20}{0.05} = 2,184.00\text{M USD} \implies V_{\text{Equity}} = 2,184.00 - 400.00 = 1,784.00\text{M USD}$$

Exam Trap: Ignore non-cash share-based compensation add-backs if analysts treat SBC as a real economic dilution cost; always add back after-tax interest to Net Income/CFO when computing FCFF, NEVER pre-tax interest.

05. Market Multiples & Valuation Ratios

Relative valuation compares a company's market price multiplier against comparable peer benchmarks or historic averages. Justified multiples are derived directly from fundamental discounted cash flow economics.

Justified Multipliers Derived from Fundamentals

Core Fundamental Justified Multiplier Identities
$$\text{Justified Leading P/E: } \frac{P_0}{E_1} = \frac{1 - b}{r - g}$$ $$\text{Justified Trailing P/E: } \frac{P_0}{E_0} = \frac{(1 - b)(1 + g)}{r - g}$$ $$\text{Justified Price-to-Book (P/B): } \frac{P_0}{B_0} = \frac{ROE - g}{r - g}$$ $$\text{Justified Price-to-Sales (P/S): } \frac{P_0}{S_0} = \frac{\text{Net Margin} \times (1 - b)(1 + g)}{r - g}$$

Enterprise Value Multipliers, Harmonic Mean & Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP)

Enterprise Value ($EV$) measures total market value of core operations independent of capital structure financing:

Enterprise Value, PEG & Harmonic Mean Identities
$$EV = \text{Market Value of Common Equity} + \text{Total Debt} + \text{Preferred Stock} + \text{Minority Interest} - \text{Cash & Equivalents}$$ $$\text{PEG Ratio} = \frac{P/E}{g \text{ (in whole \%)}}, \qquad \bar{X}_{H} = \frac{n}{\sum_{i=1}^n (1 / X_i)}$$

CFA Exam Rule on Peer Multiples: When averaging peer P/E multiples containing high positive outliers (e.g., depressed earnings), the Harmonic Mean mitigates extreme upward skew compared to the arithmetic mean. For diversified conglomerates, use Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP) valuation—applying distinct EV/EBITDA or P/E multiples to each operating segment before subtracting net debt.

Worked Numerical Example 8: Justified Multiples & Harmonic Mean Benchmark

Part A (Justified Fundamentals): A company earns $ROE = 15.0\%$, retains $b = 40\%$ ($g = 0.40 \times 15.0\% = 6.0\%$), and has required return $r = 10.0\%$.

  • Justified Leading P/E: $\frac{1 - 0.40}{0.10 - 0.06} = \frac{0.60}{0.04} = 15.00\times$. Justified Trailing P/E: $15.00 \times 1.06 = 15.90\times$.
  • Justified P/B: $\frac{ROE - g}{r - g} = \frac{0.15 - 0.06}{0.10 - 0.06} = 2.25\times$.

Part B (Harmonic vs. Arithmetic Mean): Three industry peers trade at P/E ratios of $12.0\times$, $15.0\times$, and $60.0\times$.

$$\text{Arithmetic Mean} = \frac{12 + 15 + 60}{3} = 29.00\times, \qquad \text{Harmonic Mean } \bar{X}_H = \frac{3}{\frac{1}{12} + \frac{1}{15} + \frac{1}{60}} = \frac{3}{0.1667} = 18.00\times$$

06. Residual Income Valuation & Clean Surplus Accounting

Residual Income (Economic Profit) equals net income in excess of the accounting equity charge for the cost of equity capital ($r \times B_{t-1}$).

Residual Income Valuation Equation

Universal Residual Income Model
$$V_0 = B_0 + \sum_{t=1}^\infty \frac{RI_t}{(1 + r)^t}$$ $$RI_t = NI_t - (r \times B_{t-1}) = (ROE_t - r) \times B_{t-1}$$
Clean Surplus Relation: The balance sheet book value must reconcile perfectly through earnings and dividends: $$B_t = B_{t-1} + NI_t - Div_t$$ Items bypassing the income statement through Other Comprehensive Income (AOCI)—such as foreign currency translation adjustments or unrealized gains on OCI securities—violate clean surplus and require analyst normalization.

Continuing Residual Income, Persistence ($\omega$) & EVA / MVA

Under competitive market dynamics, supernormal returns decay over time to normal returns ($ROE \to r$). Related economic metrics include Economic Value Added (EVA) $= NOPAT - (WACC \times \text{Invested Capital})$ and Market Value Added (MVA) $= \text{Market Value} - \text{Invested Capital}$:

Terminal Value with Persistence Decay ($\omega$)
$$PV(\text{Continuing RI at } T-1) = \frac{RI_T}{1 + r - \omega}$$ $$\text{Where } \omega \in [0, 1]: \quad \omega = 1.0 \implies \text{Perpetual RI}, \quad \omega = 0.0 \implies \text{Instant Return to Cost of Equity}$$

Worked Numerical Example 9: Multi-Stage Residual Income with Persistence Decay

Scenario: Beginning book value $B_0 = 20.00\text{ USD}$, cost of equity $r = 10.0\%$. Forecasts: Year 1 ($EPS_1 = 4.00, D_1 = 1.00$), Year 2 ($EPS_2 = 4.50, D_2 = 1.20$), Year 3 ($EPS_3 = 4.80, D_3 = 1.30$). After Year 3, residual income decays with persistence factor $\omega = 0.60$.

  1. Year 1: $RI_1 = 4.00 - 0.10(20.00) = 2.00\text{ USD}$; $B_1 = 20.00 + 4.00 - 1.00 = 23.00\text{ USD}$.
  2. Year 2: $RI_2 = 4.50 - 0.10(23.00) = 2.20\text{ USD}$; $B_2 = 23.00 + 4.50 - 1.20 = 26.30\text{ USD}$.
  3. Year 3: $RI_3 = 4.80 - 0.10(26.30) = 2.17\text{ USD}$.
  4. PV of Continuing RI at $t=2$: $\frac{RI_3}{1 + r - \omega} = \frac{2.17}{1 + 0.10 - 0.60} = \frac{2.17}{0.50} = 4.34\text{ USD}$.
  5. Intrinsic Equity Value ($V_0$): $$V_0 = 20.00 + \frac{2.00}{1.10} + \frac{2.20 + 4.34}{(1.10)^2} = 20.00 + 1.818 + 5.405 = 27.22\text{ USD}$$

07. Private Company Valuation & Normalization Adjustments

Private company valuation requires adjusting financial statements for non-market transactions and quantifying illiquidity and control differentials.

Financial Statement Normalization

  • Officer/Owner Compensation: Replace non-market excessive or below-market salaries with fair market executive compensation.
  • Non-Operating Assets & Expenses: Segregate excess cash, corporate personal aircraft, or real estate assets held for speculation.
  • Related-Party Transactions: Re-align intercompany lease agreements to market rents.

Private Company Methods: Excess Earnings Method (EEM), GPCM, GTM & Discounts

Private equity appraisals apply three main approaches: Income Approach (FCF and the Excess Earnings Method [EEM] for intangible-heavy firms), Market Approach (Guideline Public Company Method [GPCM], Guideline Transactions Method [GTM], and Prior Transaction Method [PTM]), and Asset-Based Approach (adjusted book value).

Control and Marketability Discount Interaction
$$\text{Total Combined Discount} = 1 - (1 - DLOC) \times (1 - DLOM)$$ $$DLOC = 1 - \left( \frac{1}{1 + \text{Control Premium}} \right)$$ $$\text{Where: } DLOC = \text{Discount for Lack of Control}, \quad DLOM = \text{Discount for Lack of Marketability}$$

Worked Numerical Example 10: Excess Earnings Method (EEM) Private Firm Valuation

Scenario: A private firm generates normalized EBIT $= 360,000\text{ USD}$ (25% tax rate). Working capital $= 500,000\text{ USD}$ (6.0% required return), Fixed Assets $= 1,200,000\text{ USD}$ (10.0% required return), intangible asset capitalization rate $= 18.0\%$, and perpetual intangible growth $= 3.0\%$.

  1. Normalized NOPAT: $360,000 \times (1 - 0.25) = 270,000\text{ USD}$.
  2. Required Returns on Tangible Assets: $WC = 500,000 \times 0.06 = 30,000\text{ USD}$; $FA = 1,200,000 \times 0.10 = 120,000\text{ USD}$.
  3. Residual Intangible Income: $270,000 - (30,000 + 120,000) = 120,000\text{ USD}$.
  4. Intangible Value & Total Firm Value: $$V_{\text{Intangible}} = \frac{120,000(1.03)}{0.18 - 0.03} = 824,000\text{ USD} \implies V_{\text{Firm}} = 500,000 + 1,200,000 + 824,000 = 2,524,000\text{ USD}$$

Worked Numerical Example 11: DLOC & DLOM Multiplicative Discounting

Scenario: A controlling equity stake in a private company is valued at 10,000,000 USD. Guideline transaction control premiums average 25.0%, and the estimated DLOM is 15.0%. Value a non-controlling, non-marketable minority stake.

  1. Discount for Lack of Control ($DLOC$): $1 - \frac{1}{1 + 0.25} = 1 - 0.80 = 20.0\%$.
  2. Total Multiplicative Discount: $1 - (1 - 0.20)(1 - 0.15) = 1 - (0.80 \times 0.85) = 1 - 0.68 = 32.0\%$ (NOT $20\% + 15\% = 35\%$!).
  3. Implied Minority Non-Marketable Value: $10,000,000 \times (1 - 0.32) = 6,800,000\text{ USD}$.
